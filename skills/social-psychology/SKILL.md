## 1. Core Epistemology & Diagnostic Architecture

Social psychology is the scientific study of how people **think about** (Social Thinking), **influence** (Social Influence), and **relate to** (Social Relations) one another. Whenever analyzing a human behavioral problem, organizational dysfunction, persuasion campaign, or interpersonal conflict, apply Myers' **7 Foundational Principles**:

1. **We Construct Our Social Reality**: Objective events are filtered through attributions, expectations, and self-schemas. Two actors witnessing the same behavior often construct opposing causal stories.
2. **Our Social Intuitions Are Powerful, Sometimes Perilous**: Dual-processing (System 1 automatic/implicit vs. System 2 controlled/explicit) enables rapid adaptation but systematically generates hindsight bias, overconfidence, availability errors, and fundamental attribution errors.
3. **Social Influences Shape Behavior**: The power of the immediate situation (norms, roles, authority, group polarization, anonymity) routinely overrides individual personality dispositions.
4. **Personal Attitudes and Dispositions Also Shape Behavior**: Internal attitudes predict behavior when external pressures are minimized, when the attitude is specific to the behavior (Theory of Planned Behavior), and when self-awareness is triggered.
5. **Social Behavior Is Biologically Rooted**: Evolutionary natural selection predisposes adaptive universals (kin protection, reciprocal exchange, status sensitivity), while neuroendocrine triggers (testosterone, serotonin, cortisol, oxytocin) modulate immediate responses.
6. **Feelings and Actions Toward People Are Sometimes Negative and Sometimes Positive**: Ingroup categorization, scarcity, and aversive heat/frustration breed prejudice and aggression; proximity, similarity, empathy, and superordinate goals breed intimacy and altruism.
7. **Social Psychology's Principles Apply to Everyday Life**: The same experimental mechanisms govern clinical depression, courtroom eyewitness errors, organizational decision failures, and ecological sustainability.

## 3. Master Execution Workflow (4-Step Diagnostic & Intervention Loop)

Whenever executing a social-psychological task, follow this structured 4-step workflow:

### Step 1: Situational & Dual-Process Triage
1. Map the **Immediate Situation**: Identify physical/social proximity, group size, unanimity, authority presence, anonymity, time pressure, and incentive structure.
2. Map the **Actor's Construal & Processing Mode**: Determine whether targets are operating in **System 1** (fast, automatic, heuristic, peripheral, emotionally aroused, cognitively busy) or **System 2** (deliberative, central route, high personal involvement, high self-awareness).
3. Load the relevant capability card(s) from `references/capabilities/` using `get_skill_resources`.

### Step 2: Mechanism Diagnosis (Causal Chain Mapping)
Trace the problem using the exact experimental paradigms and boundary conditions in the loaded capability card:
- **Social Thinking Check**: Are actors falling prey to hindsight bias, overconfidence, confirmation bias, availability heuristic, regression fallacy, Fundamental Attribution Error, or self-serving bias?
- **Social Influence Check**: Is behavior driven by normative conformity, informational conformity, gradual foot-in-the-door escalation, insufficient justification (cognitive dissonance), or overjustification of intrinsic motivation?
- **Group & Intergroup Check**: Are teams suffering from social loafing, group polarization, Janis's groupthink, minimal-group ingroup favoritism, stereotype threat, or mirror-image conflict perception?

### Step 3: Evidence-Based Intervention Design
Construct concrete, actionable protocols grounded in the capability card's **Execution Steps** and **Threshold Rules**:
- Replace vague advice ("communicate better", "be less biased", "motivate people") with operational behavioral mechanics:
  - Specify **exact structural triggers** (e.g., anonymous pre-voting before group discussion, devil's advocate assignment, individual output metering, sequential blind lineups, specific 1-to-1 bystander assignment).
  - Specify **exact framing and incentive calibrations** (e.g., Mildly Pleasurable / Minimal Sufficient Justification to internalize norms; High-Efficacy Action Plan paired with fear appeals; Osgood's GRIT announced small conciliatory gesture).

### Step 4: Failure-Mode & Ethical Verification
Before delivering your output, audit the proposed analysis or intervention against the **Failure Modes & Audit Checklist** in the capability card:
- Ensure no **Overjustification Trap** (do not attach salient external payoffs to already-intrinsic behaviors).
- Ensure no **Boomerang Reactance** (do not use coercive high-threat language that triggers psychological reactance or external compliance without internalization).
- Ensure no **Catharsis Fallacy** (never recommend venting anger or aggressive outlets to reduce hostility).
- Ensure no **Tokenistic Contact** (never recommend unequal-status or competitive intergroup contact without shared superordinate interdependence).
