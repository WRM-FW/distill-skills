# Capability 07: Speed, Agility, and Speed-Endurance Development

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 17 (Movement Mechanics, Running Speed, Agility, Methods of Developing Speed and Agility, Program Design, `Table 17.1`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Running Speed**: Interaction of **stride length** and **stride frequency** (`Speed = Stride Length x Stride Frequency`).
    - Early speed gains in novices stem from increased stride length; elite maximum velocity is driven primarily by **stride frequency** and vertical ground reaction force applied during a minimal ground-contact support phase (~0.09-0.10 s).
  - **Impulse (`Impulse = Force x Time`)**: Because ground contact time at top speed is extremely brief, athletes must maximize **Rate of Force Development (RFD)** to generate large vertical/horizontal impulse within a narrow time window.
  - **Flight vs. Support Phase**: Each stride cycle consists of a **support phase** (braking/eccentric absorption from foot strike to midstride + propulsion/concentric push-off from midstride to toe-off) and a **flight phase** (recovery swing of trailing leg + ground preparation of lead leg).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Sprinting Technical Error Diagnosis and Correction (`Table 17.1`)

| Phase & Technical Error | Root Cause | Prescribed Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Start/Acceleration**: Hands too wide in 4-point stance | Misunderstanding starting stance | Place hands at shoulder-width |
| **Start/Acceleration**: Front leg 90-deg angle not achieved | Hips too high or too low in set position | Adjust hip height in set position |
| **Start/Acceleration**: "Jumped" first stride | Push-off angle too high; upward thrust too steep | Increase forward trunk lean; keep head aligned; accelerate rear leg action |
| **Start/Acceleration**: Premature upright posture | Inadequate push-off force; head raised too early | Increase push-off force; maintain forward lean; focus eyes a few meters ahead |
| **Max Velocity**: Insufficient push-off extension | Inadequate power; hurried push-off | Strengthen ankle/hip extension; practice full triple extension |
| **Max Velocity**: Foot strike ahead of center of gravity (overstriding) | Late/un-accelerated downward leg swing | Actively "paw" (accelerate) the lead leg backward prior to touchdown |
| **Max Velocity**: "Bouncing" with vertical swaying | Push-off force directed too vertically | Direct push-off more horizontally; increase stride rate |
| **Max Velocity**: Wide/flat lead-leg swing (insufficient knee lift) | Trunk/hip flexor weakness; poor relaxation | High-knee running drills (A-skips/marches) with dorsal flexion of ankle |
| **Max Velocity**: Ineffective arm action (side-to-side crossing) | Excessive shoulder rotation or trunk counter-rotation | Drive hands from hip pocket to cheek in sagittal plane; relax shoulders |
| **Max Velocity**: Head/neck hyperextended | Fatigue; improper posture | Maintain neutral erect head carriage with eyes focused straight ahead |

### 2. Three-Tier Training Method Hierarchy (`Chapter 17`)

1. **Primary Method**: Execution of sound movement technique at controlled (submaximal) progressing to maximal velocities.
2. **Secondary Methods**:
   - **Sprint Resistance** (gravity-resisted uphill <=7 deg, sled towing, parachute, weighted vest): Increases stride length and strength-speed; **load must not reduce maximal sprint velocity by >10%** (otherwise sprinting mechanics are altered: excessive forward lean, shortened stride, incomplete recovery).
   - **Sprint Assistance** (high-speed towing, downhill running at <=3-7 deg, high-speed treadmill): Increases stride frequency via overspeed neural pacing; keep assisted velocity within **105-110% of maximal unassisted speed** to avoid braking overstriding.
3. **Tertiary Methods**: Flexibility/mobility, maximal strength, reactive plyometrics, and metabolic speed-endurance conditioning.

### 3. Agility Mechanics (`Chapter 17`)

- **Deceleration + Reacceleration**: Effective change of direction requires lowering the **center of mass**, increasing braking impulse over the last 1-3 steps, leaning the trunk toward the intended new direction (planting the outside foot outside the base of support), and explosively reaccelerating.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Correcting Overstriding in Maximum-Velocity Sprinting (`Chapter 17`)

- **Problem**: An athlete reaches out with the lower leg to lengthen stride, planting the foot well ahead of the center of gravity and creating a large braking impulse during early support.
- **Application**: Cue active hip extension ("pawing" the foot backward before ground contact) so touchdown occurs just in front of the center of mass, minimizing eccentric braking time.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Diagnose Phase Bottleneck**: Separate Start/Acceleration (0-20 m, horizontal impulse, forward lean, stride length) from Maximum Velocity (20-60 m, upright posture, minimal ground contact time, stride frequency) and Agility (braking + plant angle + reacceleration).
2. **Audit Technical Faults**: Match observed mechanics against `Table 17.1` and prescribe targeted corrective drills.
3. **Structure Session & Methods**: Place Primary speed/agility drills at the very start of the workout after a dynamic warm-up with full phosphagen recovery (1:12-1:20 W:R); cap resisted sled loads at <=10% velocity loss.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Heavy Sled Towing >10% Velocity Loss)**: Towing excessive weight alters sprinting kinematics into a slow marching grind, reinforcing slow ground contact times.
- **Failure Mode (Running Speed Drills in a Fatigued State)**: Training maximal speed at the end of practice trains slow motor unit firing patterns and increases hamstring strain risk.
