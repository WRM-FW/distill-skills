# Capability 06: Plyometric Training Mechanics and Program Design

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 16 (Plyometric Mechanics and Physiology, Program Design, Age/Safety Considerations, `Tables 16.1-16.7`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Mechanical Model**: Rapid eccentric stretch stores elastic energy in the **Series Elastic Component (SEC)** (tendons and cross-bridge actin-myosin complexes); if immediately followed by a concentric muscle action, stored elastic energy adds to concentric force production.
  - **Neurophysiological Model**: Rapid stretch stimulates **muscle spindles**, sending Type Ia afferent signals to the spinal cord that synapse with alpha motor neurons, evoking a reflexive concentric contraction (**stretch reflex**) in the agonist muscle.
  - **Three Phases of the Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC, `Table 16.1`)**:
    1. **Phase I - Eccentric**: Pre-stretch of agonist muscle; elastic energy stored in SEC; muscle spindles stimulated.
    2. **Phase II - Amortization (Transition)**: Pause between the end of Phase I and the start of Phase III; Type Ia afferents synapse with alpha motor neurons. **Crucial determinant**: If the amortization phase lasts too long, stored elastic energy dissipates as heat and the stretch reflex fails to potentiate concentric force.
    3. **Phase III - Concentric**: Shortening of agonist fibers; stored elastic energy released from SEC + alpha motor neurons fire to produce explosive power.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Lower-Body Drill Hierarchy & Intensity Factors (`Tables 16.2-16.3`)

- **Six Lower-Body Drill Categories** (generally progressing in intensity):
  1. **Jumps in Place** (vertical emphasis, repeated without rest between jumps; e.g., two-foot ankle hop, squat jump, tuck jump).
  2. **Standing Jumps** (maximal single vertical/horizontal efforts with brief recovery between reps; e.g., vertical jump, jump over barrier, standing long jump).
  3. **Multiple Hops and Jumps** (repeated combinations of jumps in place and standing jumps; e.g., double-leg hop, front barrier hop, zigzag hop).
  4. **Bounds** (exaggerated horizontal/vertical speed drills covering **>30 m [98 ft]**; e.g., skip, alternate-leg bound).
  5. **Box Drills** (jumping onto or off boxes 6-42 in high; increases intensity of multiple hops/jumps).
  6. **Depth Jumps** (stepping off a box, landing, and immediately jumping vertically, horizontally, or to a second box; highest intensity).
- **Four Intensity Modifiers (`Table 16.3`)**:
  1. **Points of Contact**: Single-leg drills place far greater ground reaction force on muscles/joints than double-leg drills.
  2. **Speed**: Greater movement speed increases drill intensity.
  3. **Height of the Drill**: Higher center of gravity (higher jump or higher box) increases landing force.
  4. **Body Weight**: Greater body mass (or external weight vest) increases joint stress.

### 2. Quantitative Prescription Variables (`Tables 16.4-16.6`)

- **Frequency & Inter-Session Recovery**: **1-3 sessions/week** (up to 2-4 in off-season track/field), governed by a mandatory **48 to 72 hours (2-4 days)** recovery between plyometric sessions. Never perform plyometric drills for the same body area two days in a row.
- **Intra-Session Recovery (Work-to-Rest Ratio)**: **1:5 to 1:10 W:R ratio**; rest **5-10 seconds between repetitions** (e.g., depth jumps) and **2-3 minutes between sets**. Plyometrics are power training, never cardiorespiratory conditioning.
- **Volume (Foot Contacts per Session, `Table 16.4`)**:
  - **Beginner** (no experience): **80 to 100 contacts/session**
  - **Intermediate** (some experience): **100 to 120 contacts/session**
  - **Advanced** (considerable experience): **120 to 140 contacts/session** (for lower-intensity drills, volume may reach 200-400 contacts).
- **Program Length**: Typically **6 to 10 weeks** (vertical jump improvements appear by week 4).

### 3. Safety Prerequisites and Depth Jump Limits (`Table 16.7`)

- **Strength Prerequisites**:
  - **Lower Body**: 1RM Back Squat **>= 1.5x body weight** AND **Speed Squat** of **5 reps at 60% body weight in <= 5 seconds**.
  - **Upper Body**: 1RM Bench Press **>= 1.0x body weight** (if body weight **> 220 lb [100 kg]**) or **>= 1.5x body weight** (if body weight **< 220 lb [100 kg]**), OR **5 clap push-ups in a row**.
- **Balance Prerequisites (`Table 16.7`, held for 30 seconds on training surface)**:
  - Beginner plyometrics: Double-leg and single-leg **standing** balance.
  - Intermediate plyometrics: Double-leg and single-leg **quarter squat** balance.
  - Advanced plyometrics: Double-leg and single-leg **half squat** balance.
- **Body Weight & Depth Jump Box Height Rules**:
  - Standard depth jump box height: **16 to 42 inches (41-107 cm)**, with **30 to 32 inches (76-81 cm)** as the norm.
  - Athletes weighing **> 220 lb (100 kg)**: Avoid high-volume, high-intensity plyometrics; restrict depth jump box height to **<= 18 inches (46 cm)**.
- **Integrating Resistance Training & Plyometrics (`Table 16.6`)**: Avoid heavy lower-body resistance training and high-intensity lower-body plyometrics on the same day (unless executing advanced **Complex Training** with full recovery). Alternate high/low intensity across upper/lower regions (e.g., Mon: High Upper RT + Low Lower Plyo; Tue: Low Lower RT + High Upper Plyo; Thu: Low Upper RT + High Lower Plyo; Fri: High Lower RT + Low Upper Plyo).

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Trunk Plyometric Modification (`Figures 16.4-16.5`)

- **Problem**: Standard full-ROM medicine ball sit-ups take too long, allowing elastic energy to dissipate and preventing stretch-reflex potentiation of the abdominals.
- **Application**: Shorten the range of motion to a rapid, small-angle rebound (`plyometric sit-up`) immediately upon catching the medicine ball so the amortization phase stays brief.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Screen Safety & Readiness**: Check athlete body weight (>220 lb restriction), 1RM squat/bench ratios, 5-rep/5-s speed squat, and 30-s balance tests (`Table 16.7`).
2. **Assign Foot-Contact Volume & Drill Modes**: Select drill categories (`Table 16.2`) and total contacts/session (`Table 16.4`: 80-100 / 100-120 / 120-140) based on experience.
3. **Set Recovery & Weekly Integration**: Enforce 1:5-1:10 W:R (5-10 s between depth jump reps, 2-3 min between sets), 48-72 hr between sessions, and alternate upper/lower high-low days (`Table 16.6`).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Prepubescent Depth Jumps)**: High-intensity depth jumps and drop jumps are **contraindicated** for prepubescent children due to open epiphyseal growth plates (`Chapter 16`).
- **Failure Mode (48-Inch Depth Jumps)**: Stepping off boxes >42 in forces an extended amortization phase to absorb impact, nullifying the SSC and risking knee/ankle injury.
