# Capability 02: Biomechanics and Anatomical Movement Analysis

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 1 (Neuromuscular System), Chapter 4 (Musculoskeletal Levers, Strength/Power, Sources of Resistance, Joint Biomechanics, `Table 4.1`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Lever Systems**:
    - **First-Class Lever**: Muscle force (`F_M`) and resistive force (`F_R`) act on opposite sides of the fulcrum (e.g., forearm extension via triceps at elbow; head extension on cervical spine).
    - **Second-Class Lever**: `F_M` and `F_R` act on the same side of the fulcrum with `F_M` acting through a longer moment arm (`M_M > M_R`, Mechanical Advantage > 1.0; e.g., plantarflexion calf raise).
    - **Third-Class Lever**: `F_M` and `F_R` act on the same side with `F_M` acting through a shorter moment arm (`M_M < M_R`, Mechanical Advantage < 1.0; e.g., elbow flexion via biceps). Most skeletal muscles operate at `MA < 1.0`, sacrificing force advantage to gain movement speed and distal range of motion.
  - **Torque & Sticking Point**: Resistive torque (`T_R = F_R x horizontal moment arm`) peaks when a limb is horizontal (perpendicular to gravity), whereas muscle force capability varies with sarcomere length-tension (optimal at resting length) and force-velocity curves (concentric force drops as velocity increases; eccentric force exceeds isometric and concentric force). The **sticking point** occurs shortly after the transition from eccentric to concentric phase where mechanical advantage is poorest.
  - **Anatomical Planes**: Sagittal (flexion/extension), Frontal (abduction/adduction, inversion/eversion), Transverse (internal/external rotation, horizontal abduction/adduction, pronation/supination).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

1. **Biomechanical Factors Governing Strength**:
   - **Neural Control**: Recruitment (size principle) + Rate coding (firing frequency) + Synchronization + Golgi tendon organ disinhibition.
   - **Muscle Cross-Sectional Area**: Force is proportional to physiological cross-sectional area (not muscle length); pennate muscles (angled fibers, e.g., gluteus medius, rectus femoris, deltoid) pack more sarcomeres in parallel for higher force, while longitudinal fibers favor contraction velocity.
   - **Tendon Insertion Variation**: A tendon inserting farther from the joint center increases moment arm (`M_M`), improving lifting torque in low-speed strength sports (e.g., powerlifting) at the expense of maximal joint angular velocity.
2. **Five Sources of Resistance & Selection Rule**:
   - **Gravity** (free weights/weight stacks): Constant mass, variable resistive torque via horizontal moment arm; demands multi-planar stabilization.
   - **Cam Machines**: Variable moment arm attempting to match human strength curve.
   - **Inertia** (`F = m x a`): Acceleration raises force early in the lift; deceleration required at end range; critical in ballistic/explosive lifts.
   - **Fluid Resistance** (hydraulic/pneumatic/water): Concentric-only resistance proportional to velocity (`laminar`) or velocity squared (`turbulent`), eliminating eccentric DOMS.
   - **Elasticity** (bands/springs): Resistance increases linearly with elongation (`F = k x x`), loading the end of the concentric phase where gravity moment arms often shrink.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Lumbar Spine Protection in Squat and Deadlift (`Chapter 4`)

- **Problem**: Lifting with a rounded lower back shifts load from musculature to posterior spinal ligaments and intervertebral discs, creating uneven disc compression.
- **Application**: Maintain a neutral/slightly lordotic lumbar spine, keep the bar close to the L5/S1 fulcrum to minimize the external horizontal moment arm, and use controlled intra-abdominal pressure (diaphragm + abdominal wall + pelvic floor) rather than chronic excessive Valsalva.

### Case 2: Shoulder and Knee Joint Safety (`Chapter 4`)

- **Problem**: Subacromial impingement during overhead/lateral lifts and patellofemoral/ACL stress during deep or valgus knee loading.
- **Application**: Balance rotator cuff (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) with deltoid/pectoralis training; avoid ballistic bounce at the bottom of the squat (`wrapping` knees too tightly alters biomechanics).

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Decompose Movement by Plane and Joint**: List primary joints (ankle, knee, hip, trunk, shoulder, elbow, wrist), anatomical plane (sagittal/frontal/transverse), and muscle action (concentric, eccentric, isometric).
2. **Audit Lever & Torque Curve**: Identify where the external horizontal moment arm peaks (the sticking region) and match the resistance source (gravity, inertia, elastic, cam) to the training goal.
3. **Apply Joint Safety Screen**: Check lumbar moment arm, scapulohumeral rhythm/rotator cuff balance, and knee tracking before approving the exercise.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Confusing Mass with Work/Power)**: Lifting a heavier bar over a truncated range of motion or at very slow speed may produce less mechanical power (`P = F x d / t`) than lifting a moderate load explosively.
- **Failure Mode (Over-reliance on Weight Belts)**: Wearing a weight belt on light sets or non-structural exercises reduces natural abdominal/erector spinae activation (`Chapter 4 & 14`).
