# Capability 04: Warm-Up, Flexibility, Lifting Technique, and Spotting

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 13 (Warm-Up and Stretching) and Chapter 14 (Resistance Training and Spotting Techniques).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Warm-Up Structure (`Chapter 13`)**:
    1. **General Warm-Up**: 5-10 minutes of slow aerobic activity (jogging, skipping, cycling) to elevate core/muscle temperature, blood flow, and neural transmission speed.
    2. **Specific Warm-Up**: 8-12 minutes of **dynamic stretching** mirroring the movement patterns and velocity of the upcoming sport or workout.
  - **Stretching Modalities & Proprioceptors (`Chapter 13`)**:
    - **Static Stretching**: Hold end-range position for **15-30 seconds**; perform **post-practice/post-workout** (within 5-10 min after cessation while muscle temperature is elevated) or in separate sessions because pre-exercise static/PNF stretching can acutely depress force and power output.
    - **Dynamic Stretching**: Active sport-specific movement through ROM without holding the end position; preferred pre-workout modality.
    - **PNF (Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation)**: Uses **autogenic inhibition** (isometric contraction of the stretched muscle stimulates Golgi Tendon Organs to relax the same muscle) and **reciprocal inhibition** (concentric contraction of the opposing muscle relaxes the stretched muscle via muscle spindle inhibition):
      - **Hold-Relax**: Passive pre-stretch (10 s) -> Isometric hold of target muscle against partner (6 s) -> Passive stretch (30 s).
      - **Contract-Relax**: Passive pre-stretch (10 s) -> Concentric contraction of target muscle through full ROM against partner -> Passive stretch (30 s).
      - **Hold-Relax with Agonist Contraction**: Most effective PNF technique; combines Hold-Relax (6 s isometric of stretched muscle) with active concentric contraction of the opposing muscle during the 30 s stretch phase.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Lifting Technique Fundamentals (`Chapter 14`)

- **Handgrips**:
  - **Pronated (Overhand)**: Palms down, knuckles up.
  - **Supinated (Underhand)**: Palms up, knuckles down.
  - **Alternated**: One hand pronated, one supinated (used in heavy deadlifts and over-the-face spotting).
  - **Hook Grip**: Pronated grip with index and middle fingers covering the thumb (required for snatch, clean, and related power pulls).
  - **Closed vs. Open (False) Grip**: Always wrap the thumb around the bar (`closed grip`) for safety.
- **Five-Point Body Contact Position** (for seated or supine bench exercises):
  1. Head placed firmly on bench/pad
  2. Shoulders and upper back placed firmly and evenly on bench/pad
  3. Buttocks placed evenly on bench/seat
  4. Left foot flat on floor
  5. Right foot flat on floor
- **Breathing & Weight Belts**:
  - **Standard Breathing**: Inhale during the less stressful (eccentric) phase; exhale through the **sticking point** of the most stressful (concentric) phase.
  - **Valsalva Maneuver**: Reserved ONLY for experienced, well-trained athletes performing structural lifts at >=80% 1RM; hold breath for at most 1-2 seconds through the sticking point.
  - **Weight Belt Decision Rule**: Wear a weight belt ONLY on **structural exercises that place stress on the lower back** AND involve **near-maximal or maximal loads**. Never wear a belt on exercises that do not load the lower back or on light/moderate sets of structural lifts (otherwise core abdominal/erector spinae muscles fail to develop).

### 2. Free-Weight Spotting Rules (`Chapter 14`)

| Exercise Category | Spotting Requirement & Hand Placement | Critical Safety Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Power Exercises** (Power Clean, Snatch, Push Jerk, High Pull) | **DO NOT SPOT** | Clear a 3-4 ft drop zone; teach the athlete to push the bar away (front drop) or jump forward and release (behind-neck failure). |
| **Bar on Back / Front Shoulders / Overhead** (Back Squat, Front Squat, Shoulder Press) | Perform inside a **Power Rack** with safety pins set just below lowest depth; or 1 spotter hugging/cupping under ribcage (or 2 spotters at bar ends for heavy loads) | Never spot heavy back squats with a single weak spotter outside a rack; communicate target rep count before un-racking. |
| **Over-the-Face Barbell** (Bench Press, Incline Press, Lying Triceps Extension) | 1 spotter using an **alternated grip** narrower than the athlete's grip (Closed grip) | Spotter guides the bar on liftoff and rack-in; keeps hands near bar through the sticking point. |
| **Dumbbell Exercises** (DB Bench, DB Incline, DB Shoulder Press) | Spotter places hands on the athlete's **wrists/forearms close to the dumbbells** (or directly on the dumbbells for pullovers/extensions) | **Never spot under the elbows** on dumbbell presses (pushing the elbows can collapse the forearms/dumbbells onto the athlete's face). |

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Pre-Competition Warm-Up for a Sprinter (`Chapter 13`)

- **Problem**: A sprinter performs 20 minutes of passive static hamstring/quadriceps stretching immediately before racing, reducing neural drive and musculotendinous stiffness.
- **Application**: Replace pre-race static stretching with 5-10 min general warm-up (jogging/skipping) + 8-12 min specific dynamic stretching (walking knee lifts, lunges, high-knee marches, A-skips), and move 15-30 s static or PNF stretches to the post-workout cool-down.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Structure the Warm-Up**: Prescribe 5-10 min General Warm-Up + 8-12 min Specific Dynamic Stretching; schedule static (15-30 s) or PNF (6 s isometric + 30 s passive) post-session.
2. **Audit Lifting Setup**: Verify grip (closed/hook), stance (5-point contact for bench; hip/shoulder-width flat feet for standing), breathing (exhale at sticking point; brief Valsalva only for trained athletes on heavy structural lifts), and weight belt eligibility.
3. **Assign Spotting Protocol**: Match every free-weight exercise to the table above (Power = No Spot; Over-the-Face = Alternated Grip; Dumbbell = Wrists/DBs, never elbows; Squat = Rack/1-2 Spotters).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Spotting Power Cleans or Snatches)**: Attempting to catch a failing power clean or snatch exposes both lifter and spotter to severe trauma.
- **Failure Mode (Valsalva in Cardiovascular/Novice Populations)**: Breath-holding spikes blood pressure to >300/200 mmHg (`Chapter 5`) and can cause dizziness, blackout, or vascular danger in untrained or hypertensive individuals.
