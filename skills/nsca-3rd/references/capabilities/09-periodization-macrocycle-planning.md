# Capability 09: Periodization and Macrocycle Planning

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 5 (Overtraining and Detraining, `Table 5.3`) and Chapter 19 (Periodization Cycles, Periods, Linear vs. Undulating Models, `Table 19.1`, `Figures 19.1-19.5`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS, Selye)**: Three-stage response to training stress:
    1. **Alarm Phase**: Initial shock of new/increased stimulus causing soreness, stiffness, and temporary performance drop.
    2. **Resistance Phase**: Biochemical, structural, and mechanical adjustments (**supercompensation**) elevating performance above baseline.
    3. **Exhaustion Phase**: Prolonged excessive stress or insufficient recovery causing **Overtraining Syndrome** (sympathetic anaerobic overtraining vs. parasympathetic aerobic overtraining, `Table 5.3`).
  - **Cycle Hierarchy**:
    - **Macrocycle**: Entire training year (or multi-year Olympic cycle; typically 1 annual plan or 6-12 months).
    - **Mesocycle**: Intermediate block lasting **several weeks to a few months** targeting a specific phase.
    - **Microcycle**: Short cycle lasting **1 to 4 weeks** (typically 1 week) containing daily/weekly load variations.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Canonical Periodization Model (`Table 19.1`, Matveyev / Stone-O'Bryant-Garhammer)

| Period & Phase | Sport Season Equivalent | Intensity (%1RM) | Volume (Sets x Reps, Core Lifts) | Primary Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Preparatory: Hypertrophy / Endurance Phase** (1-6 wk) | Early Off-Season | Low to moderate (`50-75% 1RM`) | High to moderate (`3-6 sets x 10-20 reps`) | Increase lean body mass and build muscular/metabolic endurance base; low sport specificity initially |
| **Preparatory: Basic Strength Phase** | Mid-to-Late Off-Season | High (`80-90% 1RM`) | Moderate (`3-5 sets x 4-8 reps`) | Maximize force production of primary sport musculature; increasing exercise specificity |
| **Preparatory: Strength / Power Phase** (+ First Transition) | Preseason | High (`75-95% 1RM`; power lifts `75-90%`) | Low (`3-5 sets x 2-5 reps`) | Convert basic strength into high-velocity sport-specific power |
| **Competition: Peaking** (Short season / tournament, 1-2 wk) | In-Season (Single Peak) | Very high (`>=93% 1RM`) | Very low (`1-3 sets x 1-3 reps`) | Maximize peak neural output while shedding cumulative fatigue |
| **Competition: Maintenance** (Long team season, many weeks) | In-Season (Multi-Game) | Moderate (`~80-85% 1RM`) | Moderate (`~2-3 sets x 6-8 reps`) | Prevent detraining of strength/power while prioritizing sport skill/tactics |
| **Second Transition Period (Active Rest)** (1-4 wk) | Postseason (or 1-wk unloading between mesocycles) | Recreational activity | Low / unstructured | Physical and mental restoration; injury rehabilitation; prevent overtraining |

### 2. Linear vs. Undulating (Nonlinear) Periodization (`Chapter 19`)

- **Traditional (Linear) Model**: Gradually increases intensity while decreasing volume across multi-week mesocycles (`Hypertrophy -> Basic Strength -> Strength/Power -> Peaking`). Best suited for **novice/intermediate athletes** or sports with a **single major competition peak** (e.g., track & field, weightlifting).
- **Undulating (Nonlinear) Model (DUP)**: Varies intensity and volume within the same microcycle (e.g., **Monday**: Heavy Strength `4x6RM`; **Wednesday**: Power/Speed `5x3 @ 80%`; **Friday**: Hypertrophy/Endurance `3x10-12RM`). Best suited for **advanced athletes** or **long-season team sports** requiring simultaneous retention of hypertrophy, strength, and power.
- **In-Season Tournament Microcycle Scheduling (`Chapter 19`)**:
  - **2-Game Week (e.g., Thu & Sat)**: Perform heavy/moderate resistance + plyometric/interval sessions early in the week (Mon/Tue) and lighter maintenance/rest days immediately before games (Wed/Fri).
  - **3-Game Week (e.g., Tue, Thu, Sat)**: Drop plyometrics; perform brief maintenance resistance/interval work only on non-game days (Mon/Wed) with active rest on Fri/Sun.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Anaerobic Overtraining Prevention (`Table 5.3` & `Chapter 19`)

- **Problem**: An athlete trains continuously at >=90% 1RM or high volume without unloading microcycles, progressing from Stage 1 (altered neuron function) and Stage 2 (altered motor unit recruitment & sympathetic activity) to Stage 3-4 (decreased force/glycolytic capacity, mood disturbance, infection).
- **Application**: Insert a **1-week active rest / unloading microcycle** (low intensity, low volume) after every ~3-week mesocycle and a **1-4 week Second Transition** after the competitive season.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Map the Competitive Calendar**: Identify Off-Season, Preseason, In-Season competition dates (single peak vs. multi-month league), and Postseason windows.
2. **Select Periodization Architecture**: Choose Linear (novice or single-peak) or Daily Undulating (advanced or multi-peak team sport).
3. **Populate Phase Parameters**: Assign exact `%1RM`, `Sets`, and `Reps` from `Table 19.1` for each mesocycle, inserting 1-week unloading microcycles between major phases and adjusting game-week frequency (`Tournament Week A/B`).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Attempting to Hold a Peaking Phase >2 Weeks)**: Very high intensity (`>=93% 1RM`) with very low volume (`1-3x1-3`) cannot be sustained across a 4-month team sport season without detraining muscle mass and exhausting the CNS; use the **Maintenance** protocol (`~80-85% 1RM, 2-3x6-8`) or undulating microcycles for long seasons.
- **Failure Mode (Aggressive Training Immediately Post-Season)**: Skipping the 1-4 week Second Transition (Active Rest) leads to cumulative overuse injury and psychological staleness.
