# Capability 05: 7-Step Resistance Training Program Design

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 7 (Youth, Female, and Older Adult Athletes, `Table 7.1`) and Chapter 15 (Steps 1-7 of Resistance Training Program Design, `Tables 15.1-15.12`, `Figure 15.1`).
- **Core Mechanism**: Resistance training adaptation is governed by the systematic manipulation of seven interdependent program design variables in strict sequence:
  1. **Step 1: Needs Analysis** (Evaluation of the Sport + Assessment of the Athlete)
  2. **Step 2: Exercise Selection** (Core [Structural/Power] vs. Assistance; Movement Specificity; Muscle Balance)
  3. **Step 3: Training Frequency** (Training Status, Sport Season, Split Routine, Concurrent Stress)
  4. **Step 4: Exercise Order** (Power -> Nonpower Core -> Assistance; Upper/Lower; Push/Pull; Supersets/Compound Sets)
  5. **Step 5: Training Load and Repetitions** (%1RM-Repetition Relationship, 1RM/10RM Testing, Goal Assignment, 2-for-2 Progression)
  6. **Step 6: Volume** (Load-Volume `Sets x Reps x Weight`, Single vs. Multiple Sets by Goal)
  7. **Step 7: Rest Periods** (Interset recovery matched to bioenergetic and neural replenishment)

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### Step 1: Needs Analysis (`Tables 15.1-15.2`)

- **Sport Evaluation**: (a) Movement analysis (body/limb movement patterns and muscular involvement), (b) Physiological analysis (strength, power, hypertrophy, muscular endurance priorities), (c) Injury analysis (common joint/muscle injury sites and causative factors).
- **Athlete Assessment**:
  - **Training Status (`Table 15.1`)**:
    - `Beginner` (Untrained): Not training or just began (<2 months), <=1-2x/wk, low stress, no/minimal skill.
    - `Intermediate` (Moderately trained): Currently training, 2-6 months, 2-3x/wk, medium stress, basic skill.
    - `Advanced` (Well-trained): Currently training, >=1 year, >=3-4x/wk, high stress, high skill.
  - **Physical Testing & Primary Seasonal Goal (`Table 15.2`)**: Off-season = Hypertrophy/Endurance initially -> Strength/Power later (High RT priority); Preseason = Sport/Movement-Specific Strength/Power/Endurance (Medium RT priority); In-season = Maintenance of Preseason Goal (Low RT priority); Postseason = Active Rest.

### Step 2: Exercise Selection (`Table 15.3`)

- **Core Exercises**: Recruit >=1 large muscle area (chest, shoulder, back, hip, thigh), involve >=2 primary joints, and receive priority because of direct sport application.
  - **Structural Exercises**: Core exercises loading the spine directly (back squat) or indirectly (power clean).
  - **Power Exercises**: Structural exercises performed explosively (power clean, snatch, push jerk, hang clean).
- **Assistance Exercises**: Recruit smaller muscle areas (upper arm, abdominal, calf, neck, forearm, rotator cuff), involve 1 primary joint, used for injury prevention/rehabilitation and isolated muscle balance.
- **Muscle Balance Rule**: Maintain proper strength ratios across opposing agonists and antagonists (e.g., quadriceps vs. hamstrings, chest/anterior deltoid vs. upper back/posterior rotator cuff) without requiring equal absolute force.

### Step 3: Training Frequency (`Tables 15.4-15.6`)

- **By Training Status (`Table 15.4`)**: Beginner = **2-3 sessions/wk**; Intermediate = **3-4 sessions/wk** (total body = 3x, upper/lower split = 4x); Advanced = **4-7 sessions/wk** (up to 5-6x with 3-day split routines, `Table 15.5`).
- **By Sport Season (`Table 15.6`, for Trained Athletes)**: Off-season = **4-6 sessions/wk**; Preseason = **3-4 sessions/wk**; In-season = **1-3 sessions/wk**; Postseason (active rest) = **0-3 sessions/wk**.
- **Recovery Rule**: Never train the same muscle group on consecutive days (allow >=1 rest day between sessions stressing the same muscles, but not >3 days).

### Step 4: Exercise Order

1. **Default Hierarchy**: **Power exercises first** (highest skill, neural demand, and fatigue sensitivity) -> **Nonpower core exercises second** -> **Assistance exercises last**.
2. **Alternative Structures**:
   - **Upper and Lower Body Alternated**: Ideal for untrained/beginners or time-limited circuits to reduce local fatigue and shorten rest intervals.
   - **Push and Pull Alternated**: Ensures the same muscle group is never worked in two consecutive exercises (e.g., bench press -> bent-over row -> shoulder press -> lat pulldown).
   - **Supersets** (2 exercises for opposing agonist-antagonist pair, minimal rest) vs. **Compound Sets** (2 exercises for the SAME muscle group back-to-back, minimal rest; high hypertrophy/metabolic stress).

### Step 5, 6, 7: Load, Repetitions, Volume, and Rest (`Tables 15.7-15.12`)

| Training Goal | Load (%1RM) | Goal Repetitions | Core Exercise Sets | Rest Period Length |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Muscular Strength** | >=85% (Core only; Assistance <=8RM) | <=6 (Core only; Assistance >=8) | 2-6 | 2-5 minutes |
| **Power: Single-Effort** (shot put, high jump, weightlifting) | 80-90% | 1-2 | 3-5 | 2-5 minutes |
| **Power: Multiple-Effort** (basketball, volleyball, swimming) | 75-85% | 3-5 | 3-5 | 2-5 minutes |
| **Hypertrophy** | 67-85% | 6-12 | 3-6 | 30 seconds to 1.5 minutes |
| **Muscular Endurance** | <=67% | >=12 | 2-3 | <=30 seconds |

- **1RM Testing Protocol (`Figure 15.1`)**:
  1. Light warm-up: 5-10 reps -> **1 min rest**.
  2. Warm-up set 2: Add +10-20 lb (4-9 kg) or 5-10% (upper body) / +30-40 lb (14-18 kg) or 10-20% (lower body) for 3-5 reps -> **2 min rest**.
  3. Near-maximal set 3: Add +10-20 lb or 5-10% (upper) / +30-40 lb or 10-20% (lower) for 2-3 reps -> **2-4 min rest**.
  4. 1RM Attempt: Add +10-20 lb or 5-10% (upper) / +30-40 lb or 10-20% (lower) and attempt 1RM -> if successful, rest **2-4 min** and repeat step 4; if failed, rest **2-4 min** and subtract 5-10 lb (2.5-5%) upper / 15-20 lb (5-10%) lower. Measure true 1RM within **3-5 testing sets**.
- **2-for-2 Progression Rule (`Table 15.10`)**: If an athlete completes >=2 reps over the assigned goal in the last set for 2 consecutive workouts, add **+2.5-5 lb (1-2 kg)** (upper, smaller/weaker) or **+5-10 lb (2-4 kg)** (lower, smaller/weaker) / **+5-10+ lb (2-4+ kg)** (upper, larger/stronger) or **+10-15+ lb (4-7+ kg)** (lower, larger/stronger).

### Special Population Adaptations (`Chapter 7`)

- **Youth / Children**: Prepubescent strength gains are primarily neural (motor unit recruitment/coordination) without hypertrophy; avoid 1RM maximal testing in unconditioned children, prioritize technique, supervise closely, and progress gradually (epiphyseal growth plate protection).
- **Female Athletes**: Absolute upper-body strength is ~50-60% and lower-body strength ~70-80% of male values, but strength relative to muscle cross-sectional area is identical; emphasize upper-body development and ACL injury prevention (hamstring/glute/landing mechanics).
- **Older Adults**: Counteract sarcopenia and osteopenia (`Table 7.1`); start at 1-3 sets of 8-12 reps at low-to-moderate intensity, avoid Valsalva maneuver, and progress toward higher-intensity strength and power training after medical screening.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Scenario A - Female Collegiate Basketball Center in Preseason (`Table 15.2`)

- **Profile**: Advanced status, completed 4x/wk off-season hypertrophy (3 sets x 10-12RM).
- **Application**: Shift preseason goal to **Strength/Power** at 3x/wk (Mon/Wed/Fri); order Power exercises first (Push Jerk, Power Clean at 75-85% 1RM for 3-5 sets of 3-5 reps) followed by Nonpower Core (Back Squat, Bench Press at >=85% 1RM for 3-4 sets of <=6 reps) and Assistance (8-10RM).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Assigning >=85% 1RM / <=6RM to Assistance Exercises)**: Heavy <6RM loads on single-joint exercises (e.g., biceps curl, lateral raise, leg extension) overstress joints and tendons; cap assistance exercises at >=8RM (`Table 15.9` footnote).
- **Failure Mode (Performing Power Exercises After Exhausting Core/Assistance Lifts)**: Fatigue degrades bar velocity and motor coordination, turning explosive power lifts into injury hazards.
