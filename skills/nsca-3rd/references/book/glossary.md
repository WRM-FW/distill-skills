# GLOSSARY: NSCA Standardized Terminology & Equations

## Core Equations and Quantitative Definitions

- **1-Repetition Maximum (1RM)**: Greatest amount of weight that can be lifted with proper technique for only one repetition.
- **2-for-2 Rule**: Load progression rule stating that if an athlete performs >=2 repetitions over the assigned repetition goal in the last set for 2 consecutive workouts, weight should be added in the next session.
- **Amortization Phase**: Transition phase (Phase II) of the Stretch-Shortening Cycle between the end of the eccentric stretch and the initiation of the concentric push-off; must be kept minimal to preserve stored elastic energy and stretch-reflex potentiation.
- **Arteriovenous Oxygen Difference (a-vO2 diff)**: Difference in oxygen content between arterial and mixed venous blood (mL O2 per 100 mL blood), reflecting peripheral tissue oxygen extraction.
- **Cardiac Output (Q)**: Volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute (`Q = Heart Rate x Stroke Volume`).
- **Closed Kinetic Chain (CKC)**: Movement involving sequential joints where the terminal (distal) segment meets considerable external resistance that prohibits or restrains its free motion (e.g., squat, push-up, deadlift).
- **Compound Set**: Sequentially performing two different exercises for the same muscle group with minimal rest between them.
- **Core Exercise**: Multijoint exercise recruiting one or more large muscle areas (chest, shoulder, back, hip, thigh) with synergistic assistance from smaller muscles.
- **DAPRE (Daily Adjustable Progressive Resistive Exercise)**: Knight's 4-set rehabilitation loading system: Set 1 = 10 reps at 50% estimated working 1RM; Set 2 = 6 reps at 75%; Set 3 = max reps at 100%; Set 4 and next-session load adjusted by Set 3 repetition count (`Table 20.2`).
- **Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) & Karvonen Formula**:
  - `APMHR = 220 - Age`
  - `HRR = APMHR - Resting Heart Rate (RHR)`
  - `Target Heart Rate (THR) = (HRR x Exercise Intensity %) + RHR`
- **Impulse**: Product of generated force and the time required for its production (`Impulse = Force x Time`), which dictates change in momentum (`F x t = m x v`).
- **Lactate Threshold (LT) / Onset of Blood Lactate Accumulation (OBLA)**: Exercise intensity (%VO2max) at which blood lactate begins to accumulate above baseline (`LT`) and reaches 4 mmol/L (`OBLA`).
- **Load-Volume**: Total mechanical work indicator calculated as `Sets x Repetitions x Weight Lifted` (optionally multiplied by vertical displacement).
- **Mechanical Advantage (MA)**: Ratio of the moment arm through which an internal muscle force acts (`M_int`) to the moment arm through which an external resistive force acts (`M_ext`). `MA > 1.0` confers force advantage; `MA < 1.0` (most human skeletal levers) confers velocity/range-of-motion advantage.
- **Metabolic Equivalent (MET)**: Multiple of resting oxygen uptake, where `1 MET = 3.5 mL / kg / min`.
- **Open Kinetic Chain (OKC)**: Movement where the terminal (distal) joint/segment is free to move in space (e.g., seated knee extension, biceps curl).
- **Power**: Rate of performing mechanical work (`Power = Work / Time = Force x Velocity`), expressed in watts (W).
- **Power Exercise**: Structural exercise performed very quickly or explosively (e.g., power clean, snatch, push jerk, high pull).
- **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE)**: Borg 15-point scale (6-20) or 10-point category-ratio scale used to monitor subjective exercise intensity.
- **Size Principle**: Motor units are recruited in an ascending order according to their recruitment thresholds and firing rates (small, slow-twitch Type I motor units first; large, fast-twitch Type IIa and Type IIx motor units at high force/power demands).
- **Structural Exercise**: Core exercise that loads the axial skeleton (spine) directly (e.g., back squat) or indirectly (e.g., power clean), requiring torso stabilization (`erector spinae`).
- **Superset**: Sequentially performing two exercises that stress two opposing muscle groups (agonist and antagonist, e.g., bench press and bent-over row) with minimal rest.
- **Valsalva Maneuver**: Expiratory effort against a closed glottis, which increases intra-abdominal and intrathoracic pressure to rigidify the torso and support the vertebral column during heavy structural lifts (1-2 s max duration).
- **Work**: Product of force exerted on an object and the distance the object moves in the direction in which the force is exerted (`Work = Force x Distance`), expressed in joules (J).
