# Capability 08: Speed, Change-of-Direction (COD), and Agility Development

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 20 (Program Design and Technique for Speed and Agility Training, `Tables 20.1-20.12`, `Figures 20.1-20.13`, 11 Speed and Agility Drills).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Four Distinct Definitions (`Ch 20`)**:
    1. **Speed**: Skills and abilities needed to achieve high linear movement velocities (`Speed = Step Length x Step Frequency`).
    2. **Change of Direction (COD)**: Skills and abilities needed to explosively decelerate, deflect the center of mass (COM), and reaccelerate in a preplanned direction, velocity, or mode.
    3. **Maneuverability**: Ability to maintain velocity around curved paths/bends or transition smoothly between locomotion modes (e.g., sprint -> side-shuffle -> backpedal).
    4. **Agility**: Skills and abilities needed to change direction, velocity, or mode **in response to an external sport-specific stimulus** (integrating **perceptual-cognitive ability** [visual scanning, anticipation, pattern recognition, situational knowledge, decision-making time] with physical COD action capacity via **perception-action coupling**).
  - **Biomechanical Determinants of Sprinting (`Figures 20.1-20.8`)**:
    - Because athletic ground contacts last `0-200 ms` (whereas maximal voluntary force takes `>=300-600 ms`, `Figure 20.1`), **Rate of Force Development (RFD)** and **Net Impulse (`F x dt = m x dv`)** dictate performance.
    - **Acceleration vs. Maximum Velocity (`Figure 20.2`)**: Acceleration (`GCT ~0.17-0.20 s`) requires large net **horizontal propulsive impulse** with forward trunk/shin inclination (`45 deg`) and piston-like low heel recovery. Maximum velocity (`GCT ~0.09-0.11 s`; elite males reach `11.0-12.5 m/s` with `2.70 m` step length and `4.63 steps/s`, `Figure 20.6`) requires massive **vertical ground reaction force** applied asymmetrically in the **first half of stance** (deviating from the simple symmetrical Spring-Mass Model `Figure 20.3`), upright stacked posture, high heel recovery ("stepping over the opposite knee"), and active downward/backward foot velocity ("negative foot speed") prior to touchdown just in front of the COM.
  - **Biomechanical Determinants of COD and Braking (`Figures 20.4, 20.11`)**:
    - Plant-phase ground contact time in COD (`0.15-0.60 s`) and reactive agility (`0.23-0.25 s`) increases with cutting angle and entry momentum (`Mass x Velocity`).
    - **Angle Threshold**: Cuts **< 45-75 degrees** rely primarily on fast-SSC (`<250 ms`) velocity maintenance; sharp cuts **>= 75 degrees** (e.g., 5-0-5 180-deg turn) require multi-step **braking impulse** over the penultimate and final steps (`>250 ms`), high eccentric knee-extensor strength, lowered COM, co-triple flexion (hip, knee, ankle), posterior trunk lean during braking, and wide lateral foot plant to redirect the COM.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Sprinting Technical Error Diagnosis and Correction (`Table 20.1`, `Figures 20.7-20.9`)

| Phase & Technical Error | Biomechanical Root Cause | Coaching Cue & Drill Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Start/Accel**: Hips too high in crouch/set position | Misunderstanding setup geometry | Space feet `1.5-2 foot lengths` apart; drop rear shin parallel to track (`front knee ~90 deg, rear knee ~120-135 deg`, `60%` weight on front foot). |
| **Start/Accel**: Jumping first step or stepping *over* stance knee | Push-off angle too steep; premature high heel recovery | Cue `"drive/push through the ground"` and keep swing foot low (`ankle crossover` cutting across stance shin, not `knee crossover`). |
| **Start/Accel**: Premature upright posture | Inadequate horizontal push-off force; lifting head early | Keep head neutral in line with spine looking a few meters ahead; use incline sprints or sled tows (`Drills #4 Switches, #7 Incline Sprint`). |
| **Max Velocity**: Overstriding (foot strikes far ahead of COM) | Reaching out to artificially lengthen stride | Creates excessive braking impulse and hamstring strain risk (`late swing eccentric load`); cue `"drive foot down and back under hips"` (`Drills #2 A-Skip, #3 B-Skip, #5 Dribbles`). |
| **Max Velocity**: Open gap between knees at touchdown / slow leg cycle | "Pawing" horizontally or excessive back-side mechanics | Emphasize early hip-flexor **back-side catch** at toe-off and high front-side knee projection (`Drills #1 Ankling, #5 Ankle/Calf/Knee Dribbles, #6 Straight-Leg Bound`). |
| **Max Velocity**: Anterior pelvic tilt & chronic hamstring pain | Poor lumbopelvic control & late-swing eccentric overload | Maintain neutral stacked pelvis; use overhead A-skip/B-skip/straight-leg bound progressions and Nordic/eccentric hamstring strengthening. |

### 2. Resisted and Assisted Sprinting Guidelines (`Table 20.2`)

- **Resisted Sprinting** (sled towing, sled pushing, uphill incline):
  - **Track Sprinters**: Keep sled load light enough that running velocity decreases by **<= 10% to 12%** so normal sprint kinematics are preserved.
  - **Field/Collision Sport Athletes**: Can use heavier sled loads of **20% to 30% body weight** (or up to `40-50%` velocity decrement / `66-75% BW` for PAPE and initial `5-10 m` horizontal force overload, `Ch 17 & 20`), prescribed by velocity decrement.
- **Assisted (Overspeed) Sprinting** (bungee/rope towing, downhill): Limit speed to **<= 10% above maximal unassisted velocity** (`105-110%`); reserve for advanced/elite athletes to avoid defensive heel-strike braking and overstriding.

### 4. 3-Block Agility and Change-of-Direction Periodization (`Tables 20.9-20.12`)

- **Diagnostic Z-Score Allocation (`Figure 20.13`, `Table 20.10`)**: Compare athlete Z-scores across (1) **5-0-5 / COD Deficit** (`5-0-5 time - 10 m sprint time`), (2) **T-test / Maneuverability**, and (3) **Reactive Agility Decision-Making Time** (time from opponent movement stimulus to athlete's first definitive foot plant; `<0 ms` indicates successful anticipation).
- **Three-Phase Progression (`Table 20.11`, 10-15 min/session, 1-3x/wk)**:
  - **Block 1 (Linear Deceleration & Transitional Posture)**: `65% COD / 35% Maneuverability / 0% Reactive`. Linear start-stop `Deceleration Drills (#8)` (`1x6 x 5-10 m`, 30 s rest; hold split or bilateral athletic stance) + `Side-Shuffle` and `Backpedal` (`Figure 20.12`: toe-to-heel contact, low hips, flat back, no foot crossing; `1x4x10 m`).
  - **Block 2 (Lateral Deceleration & Multi-Mode COD)**: `60% COD / 30% Maneuverability / 20% Generic Reactive`. `10 m` linear & lateral decelerations + `Z-Drill` (`2x4`, 30 s/2 min rest) + `Y-Drill (#9)` practicing side-step, crossover, and split-step cuts (`3x4`, 1 min/3 min rest).
  - **Block 3 (High-Angle Braking-to-Reacceleration & Sport-Specific Reactive Agility)**: `50% COD / 25% Maneuverability / 25%+ Reactive Agility`. Modified `5-0-5` (`2x4`), `T-test` (`1x4`), `Reactive Y-Drill (#9)` responding to partner body movement (not just flashing lights), `Multiplanar Box Drill (#10)`, and `1v1 Evasion Drills (#11)`.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Basketball Player Agility Z-Score Audit (`Figure 20.13`, `Tables 20.9-20.11`)

- **Problem**: A collegiate basketball player tests at `Z = +1.0` on Reactive Decision-Making Time, `Z = -0.3` on T-Test Maneuverability, and `Z = -1.2` on the 5-0-5 COD test.
- **Method & Conclusion**: The player's perceptual-cognitive anticipation masks a severe physical braking/COD deficit. Allocate `50-65%` of agility training time across Blocks 1-3 to eccentric strength, linear/lateral deceleration drills (`#8`), and high-angle plant-phase mechanics (`5-0-5`) while maintaining perceptual skills in team practice.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Diagnose Speed or Agility Subcomponents**: Separate Acceleration (`0-20 m`), Max Velocity (`20-60 m`), Linear/Lateral Braking, Maneuverability (`T-test/L-run`), Physical COD (`5-0-5` & `COD Deficit`), and Perceptual-Cognitive Decision Time (`Table 20.12`).
2. **Prescribe Technical & Resisted Speed Work**: Apply `Table 20.1` fault corrections (`Ankling`, `A/B-Skips`, `Switches`, `Dribbles`, `Straight-Leg Bounds`, `Wickets`) and `Table 20.7` block progressions with full phosphagen recovery (`~1 min rest per 10 m sprinted`).
3. **Sequence COD -> Maneuverability -> Reactive Agility**: Progress from closed deceleration/postural drills (`Block 1`) to multi-angle cuts (`Block 2`) and live human-stimulus evasion drills (`Block 3`, `Table 20.11`).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Relying Solely on Flashing Lights/Arrows for Agility)**: Generic light/arrow stimuli (`Y-Drill Variation 1`) do not train sport-specific kinematic cue reading (opponent hip/trunk/shoulder anticipation) and cannot elicit negative decision-making times (`Table 20.12`); progress to live human opponents (`Y-Drill Variation 2` & `Evasion Drill #11`).
- **Failure Mode (Confusing Illinois Agility Test with Pure COD Speed)**: Because the Illinois test lasts `~15-20+ seconds`, improvements often reflect anaerobic/metabolic conditioning rather than rapid single-plant braking and reacceleration (`Table 20.3`).
