# Capability 10: Periodization, Overtraining Recovery, and Sport Psychology / Mental Health

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 9 (Psychological Foundations of Performance, Mental Health, Injury Psychology, and Motor Learning, `Table 9.1`, `Figures 9.1-9.5`), Chapter 22 (Periodization), and Chapter 24 (Overreaching, Overtraining, and Recovery).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Theoretical Foundations of Periodization (`Ch 22 & 24`)**:
    1. **General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS, Selye)**: `Alarm Phase` (initial soreness/fatigue and temporary drop in performance) -> `Resistance Phase` (biochemical/structural/neural supercompensation) -> `Exhaustion Phase` (maladaptation/overtraining if stressors persist without recovery).
    2. **Stimulus-Fatigue-Recovery-Adaptation (SFRA) Theory**: Larger training workloads produce greater acute fatigue and require longer recovery windows before supercompensation manifests; alternating heavy and light workloads within microcycles accelerates recovery.
    3. **Fitness-Fatigue Paradigm**: Preparedness = `Fitness Aftereffect - Fatigue Aftereffect` (`Figure 18.1`). Because fatigue dissipates faster than fitness during an unloading/deload or taper week, reducing volume while maintaining intensity unmasks peak performance.
  - **The Overtraining Continuum (`Ch 24`)**:
    - **Acute Training Fatigue**: Normal session-to-session fatigue resolving within `24-72 hours`.
    - **Functional Overreaching (FOR)**: Planned short-term accumulation of high volume/intensity causing a temporary performance decrement lasting **days to a few weeks**, followed by **supercompensation** after a taper/deload.
    - **Nonfunctional Overreaching (NFOR)**: Excessive training stress causing performance stagnation or decrement lasting **several weeks to months**, accompanied by psychological/hormonal disturbance without supercompensation.
    - **Overtraining Syndrome (OTS)**: Severe, chronic maladaptation with performance decrements lasting **many months to >1 year**, divided into **Sympathetic (Anaerobic) OTS** (restlessness, elevated resting HR/BP, beta-2 adrenergic receptor downregulation, decreased force/power) and **Parasympathetic (Aerobic) OTS** (chronic fatigue, depression, blunted maximal HR/lactate/catecholamines, immune suppression).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Periodization Hierarchy and Phase Parameter Matrix (`Ch 22`)

- **Temporal Hierarchy**: `Multi-Year Plan (2-4 yr)` -> `Annual Training Plan` -> `Macrocycle (several months to 1 year)` -> `Mesocycle (2 to 6 weeks, typically 3-4 weeks)` -> `Microcycle (several days to 2 weeks, typically 7 days)` -> `Training Day` -> `Training Session`.

| Period & Phase | Sport Season Equivalent | Intensity (%1RM) | Volume (Sets x Reps, Core Lifts) | Primary Physiological & Performance Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Preparatory: General Preparatory Phase (GPP) - Hypertrophy / Strength Endurance** | Early Off-Season | Low to moderate (`50-75% 1RM`) | High (`3-6 sets x 8-20 reps`) | Build work capacity, lean body mass, connective tissue tolerance, and motor skill base |
| **Preparatory: Specific Preparatory Phase (SPP) - Basic Strength** | Mid-to-Late Off-Season | High (`80-95% 1RM`) | Moderate to high (`3-5 sets x 4-8 reps`) | Maximize force production (`cross-sectional area + neural drive`) in sport-specific multijoint movements |
| **First Transition Period - Strength / Power Phase** | Preseason | High (`75-95% 1RM`; `30-90%` on ballistic/WL derivatives) | Low to moderate (`3-5 sets x 2-5 reps`) | Convert maximal strength into high RFD, impulse, and sport-specific movement velocity |
| **Competitive Period: Peaking** (1-2 weeks for single/major championship) | In-Season (Championship Peak) | Very high (`>= 93% 1RM` or `50-90%` explosive) | Very low (`1-3 sets x 1-3 reps`, `-40% to -60%` volume taper) | Dissipate residual fatigue (`Fitness-Fatigue`) and maximize neural/contractile readiness |
| **Competitive Period: Maintenance** (Multi-month league season) | In-Season (Team Sports) | Moderate to high (`80-85% 1RM`, or undulating `75-90%`) | Low to moderate (`2-3 sets x 3-6` or `6-8 reps`) | Prevent detraining of fast-twitch CSA, strength, and RFD using Retaining Loads (`1-3x/wk` or microdosing) |
| **Second Transition Period (Active Rest)** (1-4 weeks) | Postseason | Unstructured / Low (`< 50% 1RM` if lifting) | Very low | Physical tissue repair, psychological restoration, and rehabilitation before next macrocycle |

- **Linear vs. Undulating (DUP/WUP) vs. Block Periodization (`Ch 18 & 22`)**:
  - **Traditional (Linear) Periodization**: Progresses from high-volume/low-intensity toward low-volume/high-intensity across multi-week phases; ideal for **novice/intermediate athletes** and single-peak individual sports.
  - **Daily/Weekly Undulating Periodization (DUP)**: Rotates volume and intensity within the microcycle (e.g., `Mon: Heavy Strength`, `Wed: Power/Speed`, `Fri: Hypertrophy/Light Mix`); ideal for **advanced athletes** and **long team-sport seasons**.
  - **Block / Conjugate Sequence Periodization (`Issurin / Verkhoshansky`)**: Sequences concentrated `2-4 week` loads: **Accumulation** (work capacity/hypertrophy) -> **Transmutation** (specific strength/power/RFD) -> **Realization** (taper/competition peaking), exploiting residual training effects.

### 2. Sport Psychology, Motor Learning, and 3-Step Mental Health Referral (`Ch 9`, `Table 9.1`)

- **Arousal & Performance Theories**:
  1. **Drive Theory (`Hull`)**: Linear `Performance = Habit Strength x Drive (Arousal)`; holds only for simple, well-learned tasks.
  2. **Inverted-U Theory (`Yerkes-Dodson`, `Figure 9.2`)**: Moderate arousal optimizes performance; optimal arousal is **higher** for simple/gross motor skills and skilled/extroverted athletes, and **lower** for complex/fine motor decision-making skills and novice/introverted/trait-anxious athletes.
  3. **Individual Zones of Optimal Functioning (`IZOF, Hanin`)**: Optimal arousal is an individualized bandwidth (not always the midpoint).
  4. **Catastrophe Theory (`Fazey & Hardy`)**: When high **cognitive state anxiety** (worry) combines with rising physiological arousal, performance suffers an abrupt, catastrophic drop (`choking / yips / twisties [focal dystonia]`) rather than a smooth curve.
  5. **Reversal Theory (`Kerr`)**: Performance depends on cognitive appraisal (interpreting high arousal as pleasant `excitement` vs. unpleasant `anxiety`).
- **Attentional Styles (`Nideffer`, `Figure 9.3`)**: **Broad-External** (`Assess` field), **Broad-Internal** (`Analyze` strategy), **Narrow-Internal** (`Prepare` mental rehearsal/breathing), **Narrow-External** (`Act` focus on ball/target).
- **Motor Skill Practice & Feedback (`Ch 9`)**:
  - **Whole vs. Part Practice**: Use **Whole practice** when task subcomponents are highly interrelated (e.g., lunge); use **Part practice** (`Segmentation` [first pull -> transition -> second pull -> catch], `Fractionalization`, or `Simplification` with a PVC pipe; reintegrated via `Pure-part`, `Progressive-part`, or `Repetitive-part`) when complexity or injury risk is high (e.g., snatch, power clean).
  - **Blocked vs. Random & Variable Practice**: Blocked practice inflates acute practice performance, whereas **Random** (interleaving different jumps/skills) and **Variable** (varying box heights/distances) practice depresses acute practice scores but produces superior **long-term motor learning and transfer**.
  - **Cues & Feedback**: Keep coaching cues brief to avoid **verbal overshadowing** (`external cues` like `"push through the floor"` early in learning; `internal cues` for minor isolation refinements). Concurrent feedback boosts competition performance; delayed, fading-frequency feedback boosts long-term skill learning.
- **3-Step Mental Health Triage & Referral Protocol (`Ch 9`, `Table 9.1`)**:

| Triage Step | Assessment Criteria | Actionable Routing Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Classify ABCs** (Affect, Behavior, Cognitions, `Table 9.1`) | - **Anxiety**: Circular rumination/worry, rapid speech/eye movement, restlessness, GI symptoms, shaking.<br>- **Depression**: **Anhedonia (lack of emotion), hopelessness/helplessness**, low energy, social isolation, sleep/appetite changes.<br>- **Stress**: Urgency, agitation, inability to recover, impulsive decisions. | Document observable **Signs** (pacing, missed workouts, agitation) and self-reported **Symptoms** (fatigue, worry, hopelessness). |
| **Step 2: Evaluate Duration & Life Disruption** | - **Subclinical / Transient**: Lasts **<= 1-2 weeks** AND does **not** disrupt sleep, eating, academics, self-care, or outside social functioning.<br>- **Clinical / Persistent**: Lasts **>= 2 to 3+ weeks** OR disrupts sleep, nutrition, school, or self-care. | Distinguish sport-specific performance frustration from systemic clinical distress. |
| **Step 3: Refer to Qualified Professional** | - **Subclinical / Sport-Performance Only** (e.g., 1-wk swim slump, team intensity friction without life disruption): Refer to a **Certified Mental Performance Consultant (CMPC)** / performance coach for goal setting, self-talk, imagery, PMR, and diaphragmatic breathing.<br>- **Persistent (>=2-3 wk) or Life-Disrupting** (e.g., 3-mo insomnia/storm-outs, 6-wk depressive withdrawal/skipping school): Refer immediately to a **licensed Counselor or Clinical Psychologist** in coordination with sports medicine. | Maintain confidentiality and dignity; **Psychological Injury Recovery** (`Figures 9.4-9.5`) must pair process goals, social connectedness, and systematic desensitization with physical rehab. |

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Architect the Annual Macrocycle**: Map competitive dates, divide into Preparatory (`GPP -> SPP`), First Transition (`Strength/Power`), Competitive (`Peaking` vs. `Maintenance`), and Second Transition (`Active Rest`), and embed `3:1` or `4:1` loading-to-deload mesocycles (`Figure 18.1`).
2. **Monitor for NFOR/OTS & Prescribe Recovery**: Track jump RSI/mRSI, bar velocity, session-RPE, sleep (`>=7-9 hr`), and mood; if performance stagnates >2 weeks, insert a deload week (`-40% to -50%` volume) and audit nutrition/sleep.
3. **Apply Psychological & Motor Learning Tools**: Pair process + outcome and short + long-term goals; match arousal control (diaphragmatic breathing/PMR to lower; self-talk/imagery to raise) to task complexity; use progressive-part and random/variable practice for skill learning; execute the 3-Step ABC Mental Health Triage when distress appears.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Using Punishment for Technical Mistakes)**: Positive or negative punishment for skill errors floods working memory with task-irrelevant worry cues and narrows attention, triggering choking (`Catastrophe Theory`, `Ch 9`); use positive reinforcement and brief external cues instead.
- **Failure Mode (Adding Volume When an Athlete Is in NFOR/OTS)**: Misinterpreting nonfunctional overreaching as "lack of fitness" and adding extra conditioning volume pushes the athlete into full Overtraining Syndrome requiring months of recovery (`Ch 24`).
