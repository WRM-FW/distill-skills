# Capability 01: Bioenergetics, HIIT, and Endocrine Training Prescription

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 1 (Cardiorespiratory & Neuromuscular Systems, `Tables 1.1-1.2`), Chapter 3 (Bioenergetics of Exercise and Training, `Tables 3.1-3.6`, `Figures 3.1-3.10`), Chapter 4 (Endocrine Responses to Resistance Exercise, `Table 4.1`, `Figures 4.1-4.7`), and Chapter 5 (Adaptations to Anaerobic Training, `Tables 5.1-5.2`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **ATP Hydrolysis & Metabolic Acidosis (`Ch 3`)**: `ATP + H2O -> ADP + Pi + H+ + Energy` (catalyzed by myosin ATPase). Contrary to the "lactic acid" myth, **Robergs et al.** demonstrated that non-mitochondrial ATP hydrolysis is the dominant source of proton (`H+`) accumulation during fatiguing high-intensity exercise, whereas the lactate dehydrogenase (`LDH`) reaction (`Pyruvate + NADH + H+ <-> Lactate + NAD+`) actually consumes protons and regenerates `NAD+` to sustain fast glycolysis.
  - **Three Integrated Energy Systems (`Tables 3.1-3.3`)**:
    1. **Phosphagen System**: Rate Rank #1 (fastest), Capacity Rank #5 (lowest); dominates `0-6 s` of extremely high-intensity effort via creatine kinase (`ADP + CP <-> ATP + Cr`, driven by the **law of mass action**) and adenylate kinase/myokinase (`2 ADP <-> ATP + AMP`, where `AMP` allosterically activates **PFK**).
    2. **Fast (Anaerobic) Glycolysis**: Rate Rank #2, Capacity Rank #4; dominates `6-30 s` (with phosphagen) and `30 s to 2 min` (primary). Net yield: **2 ATP** per blood glucose molecule (uses 2 ATP via hexokinase & PFK, produces 4 ATP) or **3 ATP** per muscle glycogen glucose unit (bypasses hexokinase). Rate-limiting enzyme: **Phosphofructokinase (PFK)**.
    3. **Slow (Aerobic) Glycolysis & Oxidative System**: Rate Ranks #3-#5, Capacity Ranks #3-#1; dominates `2-3 min` (with slow glycolysis) and `>3 min` (oxidative). Rate-limiting Krebs cycle enzyme: **Isocitrate dehydrogenase**. Using modern stoichiometry (`2.5 ATP per NADH`, `1.5 ATP per FADH2`), complete oxidation of 1 blood glucose yields **32 net ATP** (or **34 net ATP** from muscle glycogen via the malate-aspartate shuttle, `Table 3.1`), while beta-oxidation of one 16-carbon palmitic acid triglyceride yields **>300 ATP**.
  - **Lactate Breakpoints (`Figure 3.5`)**:
    - **Lactate Threshold (LT / LT1)**: Abrupt baseline inflection at **50-60% VO2max** in untrained individuals and **70-80% VO2max** in aerobically trained athletes.
    - **Onset of Blood Lactate Accumulation (OBLA / LT2)**: Second inflection point at **~4 mmol/L** blood lactate (or calculated via modified Dmax), corresponding to recruitment of large Type II motor units.
  - **Substrate Depletion & Repletion Kinetics (`Ch 3`)**:
    - **ATP & CP**: ATP drops at most 50-60% during fatigue; CP drops 50-70% in 5-30 s and near 100% at exhaustion. **ATP resynthesis** completes in **3-5 minutes**; **CP resynthesis** has a half-time of **~30 seconds** (`50%` restored) and completes within **8 minutes** (driven primarily by aerobic metabolism).
    - **Glycogen**: Total body stores ~300-400 g in muscle + 70-100 g in liver. High-intensity resistance training (few sets) can deplete muscle glycogen by **20-60%**. Optimal repletion (`5-6 mmol/g/hr`) requires **0.7 to 3.0 g carbohydrate/kg body weight every 2 hours** post-exercise, achieving full restoration within **24 hours** (delayed if heavy eccentric muscle damage is present).
  - **Endocrine Manipulation (`Ch 4`)**:
    - **Testosterone**: Acutely elevated by (1) large muscle group structural exercises (squat, deadlift, power clean), (2) heavy resistance (**85-95% 1RM**), (3) moderate-to-high volume (multiple sets/exercises), and (4) short rest intervals (**30-60 s**). Pre/post-workout carbohydrate + protein attenuates initial 1-hr androgen receptor (`AR`) downregulation before 48-72 hr upregulation.
    - **22 kD Growth Hormone (GH)**: Secreted by Band 1 anterior pituitary somatotrophs in response to `H+` / lactate acidosis (**10RM loads, 3 sets, 1-min rest**). Band 2 somatotrophs secrete higher-molecular-weight disulfide aggregates stimulated by heavier mechanical loading.
    - **Cortisol & Catecholamines**: High-volume, large-muscle, short-rest protocols elevate both cortisol (acute tissue remodeling vs. chronic catabolism/immune suppression if elevated >24 hr) and adrenal medulla catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine/dopamine). Protocols must vary volume and rest periods across microcycles to prevent adrenal exhaustion and beta-2 receptor downregulation.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Interval Work-to-Rest (W:R) Ratio Prescription Matrix (`Table 3.6`)

| % of Maximum Power | Primary Energy System Stressed | Typical Exercise Time | Prescribed Work-to-Rest Ratio (W:R) | Primary Bioenergetic Limiting Factor (`Table 3.4`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **90-100%** | Phosphagen | 5 to 10 s | **1:12 to 1:20** | ATP and CP depletion (e.g., discus, 100 m sprint, repeated snatch) |
| **75-90%** | Fast Glycolysis | 15 to 30 s | **1:3 to 1:5** | Lower pH (H+ metabolic acidosis) & ATP/CP |
| **30-75%** | Fast Glycolysis and Oxidative | 1 to 3 min | **1:3 to 1:4** | Lower pH (H+ acidosis) & Muscle glycogen (e.g., 400 m run/swim) |
| **20-30%** | Oxidative | > 3 min | **1:1 to 1:3** | Muscle glycogen & Liver glycogen (e.g., marathon, triathlon) |

### 2. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) 9-Variable Design (`Ch 3`)

- **Core Objective**: Accumulate **several cumulative minutes at >=90% VO2max** (concurrent recruitment of high-threshold Type II motor units + near-maximal cardiac output/stroke volume).
- **Nine Manipulable HIIT Variables**: (1) Active bout intensity, (2) Active bout duration, (3) Recovery bout intensity, (4) Recovery bout duration, (5) Number of bouts per set, (6) Number of sets, (7) Inter-set rest duration, (8) Inter-set recovery intensity, (9) Exercise modality (running vs. cycling).
- **Benchmark Protocols**:
  - **Supramaximal Sprint Interval Training (SIT)**: 4-7 bouts of 30 s all-out cycling (~250% VO2peak) with 4 min recovery (`1:8 W:R`) 3x/wk for 2-4 weeks -> doubles endurance capacity and matches 90-120 min continuous 65% VO2peak training in buffering capacity and glycogen storage.
  - **Aerobic VO2max HIIT (`Wakefield & Glaister`)**: 30 s work bouts at **105% VO2max** with 20 s rest (`30:20 s` or `30:30 s`) maximizes time spent >95% VO2max compared to shorter/harder 20 s bouts at 115% VO2max.

### 3. Combination vs. Cross-Training vs. Concurrent Training (`Ch 3 & 5`)

- **Combination Training**: Mixing varied intensities/distances within the same modality (e.g., an 800 m runner combining LSD runs with track intervals).
- **Cross-Training**: Using a different exercise modality with the same metabolic profile (e.g., rowing or cycling for a runner) to maintain cardiorespiratory fitness while reducing impact stress.
- **Concurrent Training**: Simultaneous training of anaerobic resistance/power and aerobic endurance within the same period/day. **Interference Rule**: High-volume/high-frequency endurance training blunts **rate of force development (RFD) and peak power** far more than slow-velocity 1RM strength (`Kraemer et al.`, `Häkkinen et al.`), whereas adding heavy resistance/plyometric training to endurance runners improves running economy and 3 km time-trial performance without reducing VO2max.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Elite Cyclist Interval Duty Cycle Selection (`Ch 3`, `119`)

- **Problem**: Elite cyclists performing fixed-work-rate intervals using a `40:20 s` duty cycle reached exhaustion prematurely with lower total mechanical work.
- **Method & Conclusion**: Switching to a `30:30 s` work-to-rest ratio sustained high metabolic flux (`VO2`, lactate clearance) for a significantly longer duration and higher total work output.

### Case 2: Endocrine Protocol Contrast (Heavy Strength/Power vs. Hypertrophy/Metabolic, `Ch 4`, `Figure 4.7`)

- **Problem**: Comparing a Low-Volume/Heavy protocol (`5RM, 3-min rest`) against a High-Volume/Metabolic protocol (`10RM, 1-min rest`) across 8 total-body exercises.
- **Method & Conclusion**: The `5RM / 3-min rest` protocol maximizes high-threshold motor unit recruitment and direct mechanical androgen-receptor signaling with modest blood hormone spikes; the `10RM / 1-min rest` protocol induces severe glycolytic `H+` accumulation, triggering a massive surge in **22 kD GH, free testosterone, and cortisol**. Periodizing both styles across mesocycles optimizes receptor sensitivity and prevents adrenal exhaustion.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Diagnose Sport Bioenergetic Profile**: Map rally/play duration and intensity to Phosphagen (`0-6 s`), Fast Glycolysis (`6-30 s` / `30 s-2 min`), or Oxidative (`>3 min`) dominance (`Tables 3.2, 5.1`).
2. **Assign Interval or HIIT Duty Cycles**: Select exact `% Max Power`, work duration, and W:R ratio from `Table 3.6` (or `30:20 s` / `30:30 s` at `100-105% VO2max` for HIIT time `>90% VO2max`).
3. **Configure Endocrine & Recovery Guardrails**: Match rest periods and load (`85-95% 1RM` vs. `10RM w/ 30-60 s rest`) to the desired hormonal/receptor stimulus, prescribe pre/post-workout carb+protein (`0.7-3.0 g/kg carb q2h`), and separate heavy endurance and power sessions to avoid RFD interference.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Blaming "Lactic Acid" for Acidosis or Delayed Soreness)**: Lactate is a fuel (oxidized by Type I/cardiac fibers and converted to glucose via the liver Cori cycle) and clears to baseline within <=1 hour post-exercise (accelerated by active recovery); `H+` from ATP hydrolysis causes acute metabolic acidosis.
- **Failure Mode (Chronic High-Volume Short-Rest Workouts Without Deloads)**: Performing bodybuilding-style `10RM / 1-min rest` sessions continuously without protocol variation chronically elevates cortisol (>24 hr), downregulates beta-2 adrenergic receptors, and causes adrenal exhaustion.
