# Capability 07: Plyometric, Ballistic, and Complex Training Design

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 19 (Program Design and Technique for Plyometric Training, `Tables 19.1-19.9`, `Figures 19.1-19.5`, 55 Lower-Body, Upper-Body, and Trunk Plyometric Drills).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Ballistic vs. Rebound Classification (`Ch 19`)**:
    - **Ballistic Exercises**: Movements projecting the body or an implement into free space with minimal reactive ground-contact SSC contribution (e.g., static squat jump, box jump with step-down, medicine ball throw).
    - **Rebound Exercises**: Movements requiring rapid physiomechanical SSC ground-contact restitution (e.g., drop jumps, depth jumps, repeated hurdle jumps, bounding). Rebound exercises elicit higher muscle activation, power, and relative impulse than ballistic exercises (`Jarvis et al.`).
  - **Mechanical & Neurophysiological Models (`Figure 19.1`, `Table 19.1`)**:
    - **Mechanical Model**: Rapid eccentric stretch stores elastic energy in the **Series Elastic Component (SEC)** (primarily tendons + actin-myosin crossbridges).
    - **Neurophysiological Model**: Rapid stretch stimulates **muscle spindles** (`Type Ia` afferent fibers -> spinal alpha motor neurons; stretch reflex latency is `20-30 ms` in quadriceps and `30-45 ms` in gastrocnemius), potentiating agonist force, while progressive training desensitizes the inhibitory **Golgi Tendon Organ (GTO)**.
    - **Three SSC Phases (`Table 19.1`)**: (I) **Eccentric** (preload/stretch), (II) **Amortization / Coupling** (pause between eccentric and concentric; **must be kept minimal** - pauses of `0.35 s, 0.9 s, and 1.5 s` lose `25%, 52%, and 70%` of stored elastic energy), (III) **Concentric** (elastic release + stretch-reflex shortening).
    - **Fast SSC vs. Slow SSC (`Schmidtbleicher`)**: **Fast SSC** = ground contact time `< 0.25 s (250 ms)`; **Slow SSC** = ground contact time `> 0.25 s (250 ms)`.
    - **Active Flat-Foot Landing ("Credit Card Rule")**: Dorsiflex the ankle in flight and actively plantarflex immediately before touchdown to land on the midfoot/forefoot with high ankle stiffness, keeping the heels just off the floor (enough space to slide a credit card underneath). Flat-foot heel-crash landings prolong ground contact time and reduce RFD by ~45% (`Cappa & Behm`).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Strength-Tiered Plyometric Progression Matrix (`Figures 19.4-19.5`)

| Athlete Relative Strength Tier (1RM Back Squat Equivalent) | Low Eccentric / Concentric Emphasis (`ECC+ / CON++`) | Moderate Rebound SSC Emphasis (`ECC++ / CON++`) | High Eccentric / Shock Absorption Emphasis (`ECC+++`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: `~1.0x Body Weight` Back Squat** (Focus on landing mechanics & skill) | CMJ to box, Stair jumps | Pogo jumps, Skipping (`Fast/compliant transition`) | Depth landing (tall-to-short), CMJ and SJ landing (`Drop Freeze #35`) |
| **Tier 2: `>= 1.5x Body Weight` Back Squat** | Rebound jumps, Low hurdle jumps | Repeated CMJs, Hops, Low-to-moderate Depth jumps (`<= 30 cm [12 in]`) | Moderate Depth landings |
| **Tier 3: `> 2.0x Body Weight` Back Squat** | Moderate Drop jumps, Moderate Hurdle jumps | Moderate-to-high Depth jumps (`> 30 cm [12 in]`), Bounding, High hurdle hops | High to Very High Depth landings |

### 2. Quantitative Volume, Intensity, and Recovery Prescription (`Tables 19.5-19.6`)

- **Foot-Contact Volume per Session by Experience and Drill Intensity (`Table 19.6`)**:

| Plyometric Experience Level | Low-Intensity Drills (Contacts/Session) | Moderate-Intensity Drills (Contacts/Session) | High-Intensity Drills (Contacts/Session) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Beginner** (No experience) | **80 to 100** | `Not recommended` to **40** | `Not recommended` |
| **Intermediate** (Some experience) | **100 to 120** | **20 to 100** | `Not recommended` to **20** |
| **Advanced** (Considerable experience) | **120 to 200** | **20 to 120** | **20 to 40** |

- **Seven Intensity Modifiers (`Table 19.5`)**: (1) Points of contact (single-leg > double-leg), (2) Horizontal speed, (3) Landing technique/stiffness, (4) Relative strength-to-BW ratio, (5) Mass (athlete BW >220 lb or external load), (6) Vertical drop/jump height, (7) Directional change (multiplanar/zigzag > linear).
- **Frequency & Recovery Guidelines**:
  - **Weekly Frequency**: **1 to 4 sessions/week** (typically 1x/wk in-season, 2-3x/wk off-season), separated by **48 to 72 hours** of recovery (plyometrics can depress jump performance for up to 72 hr).
  - **Intra-Session Work-to-Rest Ratio**: **1:5 to 1:10 W:R**; rest **5 to 10 seconds between depth jump repetitions** and **2 to 5 minutes between sets**.
  - **Depth Jump Box Height Limits**: **16 to 42 inches (41-107 cm)**, with **30 to 32 inches (76-81 cm)** as the norm. For athletes weighing **> 220 lb (100 kg)**, cap depth jump box height at **<= 18 inches (46 cm)**.

### 3. Complex Training Taxonomies and Weekly Integration (`Table 19.8`)

- **Three Complex Training Structures (`Cormier et al.`)**:
  1. **Contrast Training**: Alternating high-load resistance sets (`80-90% 1RM`) and high-velocity plyometric sets set-by-set with full inter-set rest (e.g., heavy bench press alternated with clap/depth push-ups; heavy back squat alternated with box/hurdle jumps).
  2. **Ascending Complex Training**: Completing all sets of a high-velocity plyometric exercise *before* sets of a contrasting high-force resistance exercise (e.g., `2 sets` of maximal box jumps -> mid-thigh power cleans).
  3. **Descending Complex Training**: Completing all sets of a high-force resistance exercise *before* executing high-velocity plyometric sets (e.g., `3x2` heavy back squats -> `3x4` overhead medicine ball throws).
- **Split-Session Integration (`Table 19.8`)**: If not performing single-session complex training, pair **Low-Intensity Upper RT + High-Intensity Lower Plyo (Mon)**, **High-Intensity Lower RT + Low-Intensity Upper Plyo (Tue)**, **High-Intensity Upper RT + Low-Intensity Ballistic Lower Plyo (Thu)**, and **Low-Intensity Lower RT + High-Intensity Upper Plyo (Fri)**.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Youth Plyometric Prescription (`Ch 19`)

- **Context**: Prepubescent and adolescent athletes need neuromuscular control and bone/power development, but open epiphyseal plates contraindicate high-impact depth jumps.
- **Application**: Prescribe a **twice-weekly 8-10 week program** starting at **50-60 low-intensity jumps/session** (`Drop Freeze #35`, `Pogo Jump #1`, `Skip #21`, `Box Jump with step-down #32`), emphasizing anti-valgus knee alignment (`shoulders over knees, knees over toes`, `Figure 19.3`) before advancing drill complexity.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Screen Athlete Prerequisites**: Evaluate 1RM back squat relative strength tier (`1.0x`, `1.5x`, `>2.0x BW`, `Figures 19.4-19.5`), 30-s static balance (`Standing`, `Quarter Squat`, `Half Squat`, `Table 19.9`), body weight (`>220 lb [100 kg]` depth jump cap `<=18 in`), and age (no depth jumps for prepubescents).
2. **Select Drills & Foot-Contact Volume**: Choose lower-body (`Jumps in place`, `Standing jumps`, `Multiple hops/jumps`, `Bounds`, `Box drills`, `Depth jumps`) or upper-body/trunk (`Throws`, `Slams`, `Plyometric push-ups`) drills and assign exact contact counts from `Table 19.6`.
3. **Configure Recovery & Complex Pairing**: Enforce `1:5-1:10 W:R` (`5-10 s` between reps, `2-5 min` between sets), `48-72 hr` between sessions, and integrate with resistance training via Contrast, Ascending, or Descending complexes (`Table 19.8`).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Dynamic Knee Valgus on Landing)**: Inward collapse of the knees during jump landings or rebounds sharply elevates ACL and patellofemoral injury risk; regress immediately to `Drop Freeze (#35)` or box jumps with step-down until frontal-plane hip-knee-toe alignment is mastered (`Figure 19.3`).
- **Failure Mode (Turning Plyometrics into Metabolic Conditioning)**: Shortening inter-set rest below `2 minutes` or performing high-rep fatigued jumps elongates ground contact time (`>250 ms`), abolishes fast-SSC elastic return, and trains slow ground contacts.
