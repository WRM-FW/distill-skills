# Capability 05: Exercise Technique, Spotting, and Nontraditional Implements

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 16 (Exercise Technique for Free Weight and Machine Training, `Figures 16.1-16.3`, 45 Exercises) and Chapter 17 (Exercise Technique for Alternative Modes and Nontraditional Implement Training, `Figures 17.9-17.10`, 24 Exercises).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Handgrips (`Figures 16.1-16.2`)**:
    - **Pronated (Overhand)**: Palms down, knuckles up.
    - **Supinated (Underhand)**: Palms up, knuckles down.
    - **Neutral**: Knuckles point laterally (handshake position).
    - **Alternated**: One hand pronated, one supinated (heavy deadlifts, over-the-face barbell spotting).
    - **Hook Grip**: Pronated grip with thumb tucked under index and middle fingers (used in power clean, snatch, and weightlifting pulls).
    - **Closed vs. Open (False) Grip**: Always wrap the thumb around the bar (`closed grip`).
    - **Clean Grip vs. Snatch Grip**: Clean grip is slightly wider than shoulder-width (outside the knees). Snatch grip is wide, measured via (1) **fist-to-opposite-shoulder distance** or (2) **elbow-to-elbow (scarecrow) distance** with arms abducted 90 degrees (`page 469`).
  - **Five-Point Body Contact Position** (Supine or Seated Bench Exercises): (1) Head firmly on pad, (2) Shoulders and upper back firmly and evenly on pad, (3) Buttocks evenly on bench/seat, (4) Right foot flat on floor, (5) Left foot flat on floor.
  - **Breathing & Weight Belts (`Ch 16`)**:
    - Exhale through the **sticking point** (concentric transition) and inhale during the eccentric phase.
    - **Valsalva Maneuver**: Expire against a closed glottis (`1-2 s max`) to create a rigid "fluid ball" in the abdomen and air column in the thorax; reserved *only* for experienced resistance-trained athletes performing heavy structural lifts (`>=80% 1RM` or to failure).
    - **Weight Belt Rule**: Wear a belt *only* during exercises that directly load the lower back (back/front/Zercher squat, deadlift, clean/snatch pulls, standing shoulder press, yoke walk) AND involve **near-maximal or maximal loads**. Never wear a belt on non-spinal exercises or light/warm-up sets.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Free-Weight Spotting Decision Matrix (`Ch 16`)

| Exercise Category | Spotting Requirement & Hand Placement | Critical Safety & Liftoff Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Power / Explosive Exercises** (Power Clean, Power Snatch, Push Press, Push Jerk, Mid-Thigh/Knee Pulls, One-Arm DB Snatch, Log Clean) | **NEVER SPOT** | Clear lifting platform of plates/racks; teach the athlete to push the bar away/drop in front (if missed in front) or release and jump forward (if missed behind head). |
| **Bar on Back / Front Shoulders / Zercher** (Back Squat, Front Squat, Zercher Squat, Good Morning) | Perform inside a **Power Rack** with crossbars set at appropriate depth, OR use **2 spotters** (one at each end of the bar cupping/wrapping hands 2-3 in below bar ends) | For out-of-rack lunges or step-ups, 1 spotter stands close behind the athlete with hands near the hips/waist/torso to stabilize balance. |
| **Over-the-Face / Overhead Barbell** (Flat/Incline Bench Press, Seated Barbell Shoulder Press, Lying Barbell Triceps Extension) | **1 spotter** (or 2 for maximal loads) standing behind the bench using a **closed, alternated grip** narrower than the athlete's hands | Agree on liftoff verbal signal ("on three") and target rep count before un-racking; spotter guides liftoff, shadows bar without touching during set, and guides bar back onto supports. |
| **Over-the-Face / Overhead Dumbbell** (DB Bench/Incline Press, DB Fly, Seated DB Shoulder Press) | **1 spotter** placing hands near the athlete's **forearms close to the wrists** (`Figure 16.3b`), or directly on the dumbbell for pullovers/overhead triceps extensions | **NEVER spot under the elbows or upper arms (`Figure 16.3a`)**, because elbow flexion collapse will drop the dumbbells onto the athlete's face or chest. |

### 2. Key 5th Edition Exercise Execution, Verbal Cues, and Error Audit (`Ch 16 & 17`)

| Exercise | Setup & Execution Essentials | Primary Verbal Cues | Common Technical Errors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pendlay Row (`#5`, `Ch 16`)** | Feet shoulder-width, knees slightly flexed, torso **parallel to floor** with neutral spine; bar starts **at rest on the floor** each rep; pull to lower chest/upper abdomen. | `"Elbows high and wide"`, `"Reset on each rep"`, `"Hips back, not up"` | Rounding upper back; allowing torso to rise above parallel; failing to return bar to floor between reps. |
| **Zercher Squat (`#24`, `Ch 16`) & Zercher Carry (`#13`, `Ch 17`)** | Cradle barbell in the **crease of the elbows** resting on biceps, held tight to upper abdominals; retract scapulae, chest up; squat until thighs are parallel. | `"Cradle the bar in your elbows"`, `"Keep your elbows in"`, `"Control your descent"` | Allowing elbows to drift away from torso; excessive forward trunk lean. |
| **Romanian Deadlift (RDL, `#31`, `Ch 16`)** | Clean or snatch grip; start standing (after deadlifting bar up), maintain **slight static knee flexion** (~15-20 deg); push hips straight back while sliding bar down thighs to patellar tendon level (torso parallel). | `"Soft flex of the knees"`, `"Push your hips back"`, `"Slide the bar down your thighs"` | Rounding lumbar spine; letting bar drift away from thighs; actively flexing knees into a squat. |
| **Clean/Snatch Grip Mid-Thigh Pull (`#44`) & Pull from Knee-Block (`#45`, `Ch 16`)** | **Mid-Thigh Pull**: Set blocks to individualized **power position** (`knee 125-145 deg, hip 140-150 deg`, vertical trunk, straps, loads up to **140% 1RM**).<br>**Knee-Block Pull**: Set blocks at **patellar tendon height** (end of 1st pull / RDL bottom, shoulders in front of bar); transition past knees into explosive 2nd-pull triple extension + vertical shrug. | `"Drive the feet through the floor"`, `"Stay on your heels as long as possible"`, `"Shrug vertically with long arms"` | Setting mid-thigh bar at anatomical mid-thigh instead of power position (causing excessive forward lean); shrugging or flexing elbows *before* completing lower-body triple extension. |
| **Tire Flip (`#9`, `Ch 17`)** | **Shoulders-against-the-tire technique**: Feet hip-to-shoulder width back far enough for neutral spine (`~45 deg` body angle), chin/anterior deltoids on tire, supinated grip with arms outside knees -> explosive triple extension driving forward (2-3 steps) -> optional knee strike -> rotate hands to pronated push and drive tire over. | `"Drive the tire forward, don't lift straight up"`, `"Strike tire with thigh and flip hands to push"` | Feet too close to tire (rounding back); hips rising faster than shoulders; muscling/curling tire with biceps instead of driving forward (causes **biceps tendon tears**). |
| **Strongman Carries (`Yoke #11`, `Sandbag #12`, `Ch 17`)** | Brace neutral vertical spine, slight backward lean on anterior carries (sandbag/Zercher) to keep load over COM; start with **short, fast steps** and progress to longer stride length and short ground contact times. | `"Short, fast steps to start"`, `"Brace the fluid ball and push elbows forward on yoke"` | Overstriding early; lateral trunk sway or lumbar hyperextension under axial load. |
| **Forward Sled Push/Pull (`#14`) & Reverse Sled Drag (`#15`, `Ch 17`)** | **Forward Push/Pull**: Horizontal trunk and shin angle; start with feet together for heavy loads; keep front foot contact **behind the hip**.<br>**Reverse Drag**: Lean slightly backward with extended hips; step backward keeping front foot **in front of the knee** (concentric-dominant quad stimulus with minimal muscle damage/DOMS). | `"Keep front foot behind hip on forward push"`, `"Short rapid steps"` | Letting foot strike ahead of hip during forward sled drive (creates braking forces). |

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Preventing Biceps Tendon Rupture in the Tire Flip (`Ch 17`)

- **Problem**: Survey of 213 strongman athletes identified the tire flip, yoke walk, and stone lift as the highest-risk strongman exercises, with biceps tendon tears as the signature tire-flip injury.
- **Root Cause & Correction**: Standing too close to the tire and pulling vertically causes the tire to stall at hip height, forcing the athlete to curl a 100-230+ kg tire with a supinated grip. Setting the feet back, bracing the spine at 45 degrees, driving horizontally through triple extension, and rapidly transitioning to a pronated push eliminates excessive supinated elbow-flexor torque.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Establish Grip & Base of Support**: Select grip type/width (`clean`, `snatch`, `pronated`, `supinated`, `neutral`, `alternated`, `hook`) and verify 5-point bench contact or standing foot-width stability.
2. **Assign Spotting & Belt Setup**: Apply the Spotting Decision Matrix (0 spotters + clear drop zone for power lifts; 1 alternated-grip spotter for over-the-face barbell; wrist/forearm spotting for DBs; rack/2 spotters for squats) and weight belt rules.
3. **Coach Phase-by-Phase Execution**: Deliver 2-3 concise external/internal verbal cues (`Ch 16`) and audit against the exercise's Common Errors checklist.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Setting Mid-Thigh Pull Bar at Anatomical Mid-Thigh)**: Placing the bar halfway between the hip and knee forces excessive trunk flexion and places the shoulders ahead of the bar; always set the bar at the athlete's individualized **second-pull power position** (`knee 125-145 deg, hip 140-150 deg`, `Ch 16 #44`).
- **Failure Mode (Using Suspension Trainers for Maximal Absolute Strength)**: Suspension trainers (`TRX`) max out below 100% body weight (e.g., inverted row at 75 deg strap angle = `79.4% BW`; at 30 deg = `37.4% BW`, `Ch 17`); use ground-based free weights for maximal strength.
