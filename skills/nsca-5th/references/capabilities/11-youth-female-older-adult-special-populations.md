# Capability 11: Youth, Female, Pregnant, and Older Adult Special Populations

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 7 (Age-Related Differences and Their Implications for Resistance Training, `Table 7.1`, `Figures 7.1-7.5`) and Chapter 8 (Sex-Related Differences and Their Implications for Resistance Training, `Tables 8.1-8.2`, `Figure 8.1`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Youth Growth & Maturation (`Ch 7`)**:
    - Chronological age can differ from **biological maturation** by up to **5 years**. Assess somatic maturity noninvasively via **Percentage of Predicted Adult Height** (from child age, height, weight, and mid-parental height) or **Peak Height Velocity (PHV)** (`mean age ~13.8 yr in boys, ~11.9 yr in girls`).
    - The adolescent growth spurt spans **88% to 95% of predicted adult height**, with **PHV occurring at 91% to 92% of predicted adult height** (`216`). Measure growth every 3 months (or monthly near PHV). During PHV, rapid long-bone growth temporarily outpaces muscle-tendon lengthening ("stretch out before they fill out"; peak strength velocity occurs ~1.2 yr after PHV in boys), disrupting motor coordination and elevating overuse/epiphyseal plate injury risk.
    - **Preadolescent Training Adaptations**: Primarily **neural** (motor unit activation, recruitment, firing frequency, synchronization, and intrinsic motor coordination; `30-40%` strength gains in 8-20 wk) due to low circulating androgens (`testosterone 20-60 ng/100 mL` vs. `~600 ng/100 mL` post-puberty in males), though long-term (10-mo) training can modestly increase muscle thickness (`161`).
  - **Female Physiology, Pregnancy, and Injury Profile (`Ch 8`)**:
    - Relative to fat-free mass (FFM) or muscle cross-sectional area, female and male muscular strength and lower-body power are nearly identical. Females possess a higher proportion of Type I fibers and **~33% greater mitochondrial respiration**, favoring lipid oxidation and fatigue resistance in endurance events.
    - **Menstrual Cycle (`Figure 8.1`)**: Estradiol peaks in the late follicular phase (protective antioxidant/membrane-stabilizing and pro-anabolic effects on muscle); progesterone peaks in the luteal phase. **5th Edition Evidence Consensus**: Meta-analyses show **no systematic relationship** between menstrual cycle phase and strength/power performance; do *not* systematically alter resistance prescription by cycle phase alone, but individualize for athlete symptoms and sleep (`38, 50, 111, 112`).
    - **Female-Specific Risk Areas**: (1) **Noncontact ACL Injury** (`2x` relative rate vs. males in soccer/basketball/lacrosse due to dynamic knee valgus and neuromuscular deficits), (2) **Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (PFPS)** (`>2x` rate), (3) **Bone Stress Injuries / RED-S**, (4) **Concussions** (higher incidence in soccer/basketball/softball/ice hockey and longer recovery/symptom duration), (5) **Sleep Deprivation** (`<7 hr/night`), and (6) **Pelvic Floor Dysfunction (PFD) / Stress Urinary Incontinence (SUI)** (`27-36%` prevalence in female athletes, `5x` higher than male athletes and `2.7x` higher than sedentary females).
  - **Aging, Sarcopenia, Dynapenia, and Bone Loss (`Ch 7`, `Table 7.1`)**:
    - **Osteopenia**: BMD between **-1.0 and -2.5 SD** below young adult mean; **Osteoporosis**: BMD **below -2.5 SD** (`Figure 7.5`).
    - **Sarcopenia** (loss of muscle mass and function via selective Type II fiber denervation and inactivity) and **Dynapenia** (loss of muscular strength and power; **muscular power declines faster than strength** after age 30, impairing stair climbing, gait push-off, and fall recovery).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Youth Physical Development (YPD) & AMSC Prescription (`Figures 7.1, 7.4`)

- **Eight Athletic Motor Skill Competencies (`AMSC`, `Figure 7.4`)**: (1) Lower-body bilateral, (2) Lower-body unilateral, (3) Upper-body pushing, (4) Upper-body pulling, (5) Core bracing, (6) Jumping, landing, and rebounding, (7) Acceleration, deceleration, and reacceleration, (8) Fundamental movement skills.
- **Youth Resistance Training Rules**:
  - **Frequency**: **2 to 3 nonconsecutive sessions/week** (more for higher training ages).
  - **Load & Progression**: Start with unloaded dowels/PVC pipes or light loads to master technique (`RTSB` / `AMSC` grading); progress resistance gradually by **5% to 10%**. Meta-analyses show longer interventions (`>23 weeks`) and progressive exposure to `>80% 1RM` (once technically proficient) maximize youth strength and jump gains (`134`).
  - **PHV Adjustment**: When an athlete enters `88-95% predicted adult height` (especially `91-92% PHV`) or reports growth-related tightness/coordination awkwardness, reinforce movement quality, address mobility restrictions, and temporarily reduce training volume/intensity. Never dismiss persistent pain as "growing pains" - refer to a medical professional to rule out apophyseal/epiphyseal overuse injury.
  - **Avoid Early Sport Specialization**: Intensive year-round (`>8 months/yr`) single-sport specialization before puberty increases overuse injury, anterior knee pain, and burnout; encourage multi-sport sampling (`96, 131`).

### 2. Pregnancy and Postpartum Exercise Prescription Matrix (`Tables 8.1-8.2`)

| Stage | Key Physiological & Anatomical Changes (`Table 8.1`) | Prescribed Exercise Modifications & Guidelines (`Table 8.2`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Trimester** | Cardiac output `+20%`, blood volume increases, minute ventilation `+40-50%`, peripheral vascular resistance drops, metabolic rate & body temp rise, ligament laxity begins. | Continue prepregnancy activity levels if uncomplicated, monitoring intensity via **combined HR + RPE** (avoid exceeding `90% HRmax`); initiate **pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises** and **bodyweight/loaded squatting** to prepare for labor/delivery; increase water intake. |
| **Second Trimester** | Cardiac output `+40%`, peripheral resistance `-25-30%`, center of gravity shifts anteriorly, balance loss increases; **supine position compresses vena cava**, reducing venous return, stroke volume, and cardiac output. | **Avoid supine positions and exercises** (switch bench press/core work to **incline, side-lying, or standing/vertical positions**); continue pelvic floor exercises; modify exercises for balance and maternal comfort; monitor for warning signs. |
| **Third Trimester** | Blood volume `+50%` (by wk 34), slight drop in SV and rise in HR at term, increased **lumbar lordosis**, foot size increases, compression neuropathies, pelvic floor strain. | **Strictly avoid supine positions**; modify exercises to reduce lumbar lordosis strain, joint impact, and neuropathy discomfort; maintain hydration and moderate resistance training. |
| **Postpartum (First 10-24 Weeks)** | Cardiorespiratory changes normalize within `1-2 weeks` post-delivery, but **ligament laxity may persist up to 5 months** post-delivery; abdominal and pelvic floor deconditioning. | Perform **pelvic floor (Kegel) and core stabilization exercises immediately after delivery for at least 12-24 weeks**; return to impact/sport gradually (allow extra abdominal healing time after Cesarean delivery). |

### 3. Older Adult (65+ Years) Strength, Power, and Fall-Prevention Prescription (`Ch 7`)

- **Pre-Participation & Safety**: Complete medical screening (obtain physician clearance for cardiac, uncontrolled hypertension, osteoporosis, or diabetes conditions); **avoid the Valsalva maneuver**; allow **48 to 72 hours recovery** between sessions; precede workouts with a `5-10 min` warm-up; perform all movements through a pain-free ROM.
- **Strength Progression**: Start untrained seniors at `1 set of 8-12 reps @ 40-50% 1RM`; progress toward **2 to 3 sets of 6 to 12 repetitions at 70% to 85% 1RM**, **2 to 3 times per week** using multijoint free-weight and machine exercises (`76`).
- **High-Velocity Power Prescription** (*critical for ADLs, gait push-off, and fall prevention*): Perform **1 to 3 sets of 6 to 10 repetitions at 40% to 60% 1RM with high concentric velocity** (`76`).
- **Fall Prevention Rule**: Resistance training *alone* does not prevent falls (`211`); it **must be combined with progressive balance, dynamic stabilization, and flexibility training** (plus adequate protein intake) to reduce fall and osteoporotic fracture risk.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Somatic Maturity Estimation and PHV Programming (`Ch 7`, `page 392`)

- **Problem**: A 13-year-old male athlete is `165 cm (5 ft 5 in)` tall, weighs `60 kg (132 lb)`, with a mother of `160 cm` and father of `185 cm`.
- **Method & Conclusion**: Using the Khamis-Roche mid-parental height equation, the athlete is at **~90% of predicted adult height**, placing him directly inside the adolescent growth spurt window (`88-95%`) and approaching **PHV (`91-92%`)**. Increase height monitoring to monthly, prioritize `AMSC` movement quality (`Figure 7.4`), monitor for apophyseal/epiphyseal discomfort, and avoid sacrificing technical form for maximal bar load.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **For Youth**: Estimate `% Predicted Adult Height` (`<88%` pre-PHV, `88-95%` growth spurt / `91-92%` PHV, `>95%` post-PHV), build workouts around the 8 `AMSC` categories (`Figure 7.4`), progress loads by `5-10%` only when technical competency is maintained, and combine strength + plyometric landing mechanics (`2-3x/wk`).
2. **For Female & Pregnant Athletes**: Emphasize posterior-chain/glute-med/core neuromuscular control to prevent dynamic knee valgus (`ACL/PFPS`), screen sleep (`>=7-9 hr`), Energy Availability (`RED-S`), and pelvic floor continence (`UI/SUI`), and apply trimester-specific modifications (`Tables 8.1-8.2`: no supine exercises after Trimester 1; 12-24 wk postpartum pelvic floor/core rehab).
3. **For Older Adults (65+)**: Screen medical history, eliminate the Valsalva maneuver, and prescribe a multidimensional program combining **Heavy Strength (`2-3x6-12 @ 70-85% 1RM`)**, **High-Velocity Power (`1-3x6-10 @ 40-60% 1RM`)**, and **Dynamic Balance Training** `2-3x/wk` with `48-72 hr` recovery.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Supine Exercises in 2nd/3rd Trimester Pregnancy)**: Lying flat on the back after the first trimester obstructs venous return via the inferior vena cava, dropping stroke volume and cardiac output (`Table 8.1`); always incline, side-lie, or stand.
- **Failure Mode (Prescribing Only Slow-Velocity Lifting for Seniors)**: Because muscle power (`dynapenia`) declines faster than strength with aging and governs gait push-off and trip recovery, omitting high-velocity power sets (`40-60% 1RM`) and balance drills fails to maximize functional independence and fall reduction (`Ch 7`).
