# GLOSSARY: Zimbardo Psychology: Core Concepts (8th Edition) Standardized Terminology, Core Concepts, and Quantitative Anchors

## 1. All 50 Canonical Core Concepts Across Chapters 1-14

### Chapter 1: Mind, Behavior, and Psychological Science

- **Core Concept 1.1**: Psychology is a broad field, with many specialties, but fundamentally psychology is the science of behavior and mental processes.
- **Core Concept 1.2**: Six main viewpoints dominate modern psychology-the biological, cognitive, behavioral, whole-person, developmental, and sociocultural perspectives-each of which grew out of radical new concepts about mind and behavior.
- **Core Concept 1.3**: Psychologists, like all other scientists, use the scientific method to test their ideas empirically.

### Chapter 2: Biopsychology, Neuroscience, and Human Nature

- **Core Concept 2.1**: Evolution has fundamentally shaped psychological processes because it favors genetic variations that produce adaptive behavior.
- **Core Concept 2.2**: The brain coordinates the body's two communications systems, the nervous system and the endocrine system, which use similar chemical processes to communicate with targets throughout the body.
- **Core Concept 2.3**: Behavior and mental processes are produced by the integrated activity of three evolutionary layers of the brain: the brain stem, the limbic system, and the cerebral cortex.

### Chapter 3: Sensation and Perception

- **Core Concept 3.1**: The brain senses the world indirectly because the sense organs convert stimulation into the language of the nervous system: neural messages.
- **Core Concept 3.2**: The senses all operate in much the same way, but each extracts different information and sends it to its own specialized processing region in the brain.
- **Core Concept 3.3**: Perception brings meaning to sensation, so perception produces an interpretation of the world, not a perfect representation of it.

### Chapter 4: Learning and Human Nurture

- **Core Concept 4.1**: Classical conditioning is a basic form of learning in which a stimulus that produces an innate reflex becomes associated with a previously neutral stimulus, which then acquires the power to elicit essentially the same response.
- **Core Concept 4.2**: In operant conditioning, the consequences of behavior, such as rewards and punishments, influence the probability that the behavior will occur again.
- **Core Concept 4.3**: According to cognitive psychology, some forms of learning must be explained as changes in mental processes rather than as changes in behavior alone.

### Chapter 5: Memory

- **Core Concept 5.1**: Human memory is an information-processing system that works constructively to encode, store, and retrieve information.
- **Core Concept 5.2**: Each of the three memory stages encodes and stores memories in a different way, but they work together to transform sensory experience into a lasting record that has a pattern or meaning.
- **Core Concept 5.3**: Whether memories are implicit or explicit, successful retrieval depends on how they were encoded and how they are cued.
- **Core Concept 5.4**: Most of our memory problems arise from memory's "seven sins"-which are really by-products of otherwise adaptive features of human memory.

### Chapter 6: Thinking and Intelligence

- **Core Concept 6.1**: Thinking is a cognitive process in which the brain uses information from the senses, emotions, and memory to create and manipulate mental representations, such as concepts, images, schemas, and scripts.
- **Core Concept 6.2**: Good thinkers not only have a repertoire of effective algorithms and heuristics, they also know how to avoid the common impediments to problem solving and decision making.
- **Core Concept 6.3**: Intelligence testing has a history of controversy, but most psychologists now view intelligence as a normally distributed trait that can be measured by performance on a variety of tasks-both verbal and nonverbal.
- **Core Concept 6.4**: Some psychologists believe that intelligence comprises one general ability, `g`, while others believe that intelligence is best described as a collection of distinct abilities.
- **Core Concept 6.5**: While both heredity and environment influence intelligence, the heritability of intelligence within a group cannot explain differences in intelligence between groups.

### Chapter 7: Development Over the Lifespan

- **Core Concept 7.1**: Infants are born with innate sensory, motor, and social abilities that form the foundation for lifelong attachment and cognitive growth as heredity and environment interact.
- **Core Concept 7.2**: During childhood, rapid brain growth and socialization enable children to master language, progress through predictable cognitive stages, and develop emotional regulation.
- **Core Concept 7.3**: Adolescence is a culturally defined transition marked by puberty, major synaptic pruning and prefrontal reorganization, moral development, and identity exploration.
- **Core Concept 7.4**: Adult development involves navigating transitions in intimacy, career/generativity, and aging, where active cognitive, physical, and social engagement preserves well-being.

### Chapter 8: States of Consciousness

- **Core Concept 8.1**: Consciousness is a serial, subjective awareness of internal and external events that works alongside fast, parallel nonconscious mental processes.
- **Core Concept 8.2**: Everyday consciousness cycles through predictable circadian rhythms of waking, daydreaming, and multi-stage NREM/REM sleep and dreaming.
- **Core Concept 8.3**: Altered states of consciousness-including hypnosis, meditation, and psychoactive drug states-arise from shifts in attention, expectation, and synaptic neurotransmission.

### Chapter 9: Motivation and Emotion

- **Core Concept 9.1**: Motivation takes many forms, but all involve inferred mental processes that select and direct our behavior toward goals, modulated by intrinsic and extrinsic rewards.
- **Core Concept 9.2**: Motivational priorities are shaped by biological drives, unconscious dynamics, and an evolved hierarchy of needs extending from survival to parenting.
- **Core Concept 9.3**: Hunger and sex are regulated by multiple interacting biological, cognitive, social, and evolutionary systems rather than single homeostatic switches.
- **Core Concept 9.4**: Emotions are four-part adaptive syndromes (physiological arousal, cognitive interpretation, subjective feeling, behavioral expression) that motivate rapid action and social communication.
- **Core Concept 9.5**: Emotional responses are controlled by dual unconscious (amygdala) and conscious (cortical) neural pathways, autonomic arousal levels, cognitive appraisals, and emotional intelligence.

### Chapter 10: Personality

- **Core Concept 10.1**: Personality-the unique, persistent pattern of thinking, feeling, and behaving-is shaped by the interaction of biological dispositions, nonshared environments, and culture.
- **Core Concept 10.2**: Dispositional theories describe personality through biologically rooted temperaments, the Big Five traits (`OCEAN`), and personal Time Perspective (`ZTPI`).
- **Core Concept 10.3**: Process theories explain personality through unconscious psychodynamic conflicts and defenses, humanistic self-actualization, existential meaning, and social-cognitive learning.
- **Core Concept 10.4**: In everyday life, people interpret behavior through implicit personality theories, evolving self-narratives, and cultural lenses (`individualism` vs. `collectivism`).

### Chapter 11: Social Psychology

- **Core Concept 11.1**: Social situations exert powerful control over behavior through social roles, norms, conformity (`Asch`), obedience to authority (`Milgram`), and bystander dynamics (`Latane & Darley`).
- **Core Concept 11.2**: We construct social reality through interpersonal attraction rewards, cognitive attributions (`Fundamental Attribution Error`), warmth-competence stereotypes, and prejudice.
- **Core Concept 11.3**: Impersonal social systems and institutional command chains create corrupting situations (`Stanford Prison Experiment / Lucifer Effect`), which can be countered through systemic reform and Heroic Imagination.

### Chapter 12: Psychological Disorders

- **Core Concept 12.1**: Psychological disorders are judged by four indicators of abnormality (`distress, maladaptiveness, irrationality, unpredictability`) and best explained by the biopsychosocial and diathesis-stress models.
- **Core Concept 12.2**: The DSM-5 classifies mental disorders by observable symptom clusters, while emerging neuroscience initiatives (`NIMH RDoC`) map disorders onto underlying brain-network dysfunctions.
- **Core Concept 12.3**: Diagnostic labels aid clinical communication but carry severe risks of stigma, self-fulfilling prophecies, and depersonalization (`Rosenhan study`) if stripped of cultural context.

### Chapter 13: Therapies for Psychological Disorders

- **Core Concept 13.1**: Therapy across all cultures relies on a strong therapeutic alliance, a protected setting, and a credible explanatory framework that restores hope and agency.
- **Core Concept 13.2**: Psychologists treat disorders through insight therapies (psychodynamic, humanistic, cognitive), behavior therapies (exposure, operant), and cognitive-behavioral syntheses (`CBT, REBT, PPT`).
- **Core Concept 13.3**: Biomedical therapies target brain chemistry and circuitry using psychotropic medications (antipsychotics, antidepressants, mood stabilizers, anxiolytics, stimulants) and brain stimulation (`ECT, rTMS, DBS`).
- **Core Concept 13.4**: Evidence-based practice matches treatment modality to disorder type and severity, often combining CBT with medication while protecting the "worried well" from unnecessary medicalization.

### Chapter 14: From Stress to Health and Well-Being

- **Core Concept 14.1**: Distress arises from acute traumatic events, chronic societal/workplace stressors (`burnout`), and cumulative daily hassles.
- **Core Concept 14.2**: Physical stress responses unfold via the fast sympathetic SAM axis and slow HPA cortisol axis (`Selye's GAS` and `Taylor's Tend-and-Befriend`), suppressing immunity (`PNI`) when chronic.
- **Core Concept 14.3**: Stress vulnerability and resilience depend on cognitive appraisal (`Lazarus`), Type A hostility, internal locus of control, hardiness (`3Cs`), and learned optimism.
- **Core Concept 14.4**: Transforming negative stress into health and subjective well-being requires combining problem- and emotion-focused coping, cognitive restructuring, social support, sleep/meditation, and regular aerobic exercise.

## 2. Key Quantitative, Psychometric, and Experimental Reference Anchors

- **Correlation Coefficient (`r`, `Ch 1.8.2`)**: Ranges from `-1.00` (perfect negative correlation) through `0.00` (no linear relationship) to `+1.00` (perfect positive correlation). Magnitude (`|r|`) indicates strength of prediction; correlation **never** proves causation due to reverse causality or third-variable confounds.
- **Neuron Resting & Action Potential (`Ch 2.4.2`)**: Resting membrane potential `~ -70 mV`; threshold excitation triggers rapid `Na+` influx (`depolarization` to `+30/+40 mV`) followed by `K+` outflow (`repolarization`) and refractory period (`All-or-None Principle`).
- **Classic Absolute Sensory Thresholds (`Table 3.1`)**:
  - **Vision**: Candle flame at `30 miles` on a dark, clear night.
  - **Hearing**: Tick of a mechanical watch at `20 feet` under quiet conditions.
  - **Taste**: `1 teaspoon` of sugar in `2 gallons` of water.
  - **Smell**: `1 drop` of perfume diffused through a `3-bedroom apartment`.
  - **Touch**: Wing of a bee falling on your cheek from a distance of `1 centimeter`.
- **Three-Stage Memory Capacities & Durations (`Ch 5.2-5.4`)**:
  - **Sensory Memory**: Iconic (visual) `~0.25-0.5 s`; Echoic (auditory) `~3-4 s`; large raw sensory capacity.
  - **Working Memory (STM)**: Capacity `7 +/- 2` (`5 to 9`) meaningful chunks (`George Miller, 1956`); duration `~20-30 seconds` without rehearsal.
  - **Long-Term Memory (LTM)**: Virtually unlimited capacity and lifelong duration; vulnerable to retrieval failure and reconstructive distortion.
- **Prospective Memory Failure Share (`Ch 5.6.2`)**: Accounts for **~50% of all everyday memory failures** (forgetting to perform a planned future intention).
- **Intelligence Quotient (`IQ`) Formulas & Normal Curve (`Ch 6.9`)**:
  - **Classic Stanford-Binet Ratio IQ (`Terman`)**: `IQ = (Mental Age / Chronological Age) x 100` (fails in adults because mental age plateaus while chronological age keeps rising).
  - **Modern Wechsler Deviation IQ (`WAIS / WISC`)**: Standardized normal bell curve with **Mean = 100, Standard Deviation (SD) = 15**:
    - `85 to 115` (`+/- 1 SD`): **~68%** of the population.
    - `70 to 130` (`+/- 2 SD`): **~95%** of the population.
    - `< 70` (`-2 SD` + adaptive functioning deficits across conceptual, social, and practical domains before age 18): DSM-5 **Intellectual Disability** (`~2%`).
    - `> 130` (`+2 SD`): **Giftedness** (`~2%`).
- **Sleep Cycle & Circadian Architecture (`Ch 8.5`)**:
  - **Ultradian Sleep Cycle**: `~90 minutes` per cycle (`4 to 5 cycles/night`), progressing `NREM 1 -> NREM 2 (sleep spindles) -> NREM 3-4 (slow-wave delta) -> NREM 2 -> REM`. Early night cycles have more deep `NREM 3-4`; late morning cycles have longer `REM` periods (`up to 30-45 min`).
- **Asch & Milgram Quantitative Benchmarks (`Ch 11.2-11.3`)**:
  - **Asch Line-Judgment Conformity**: `~75%` of participants conformed at least once; participants yielded to the unanimous wrong majority on **`37%` (`~1/3`)** of all critical trials. Presence of **1 dissenting ally** reduces conformity by **`~80%`** (down to `~5-9%`).
  - **Milgram Baseline Obedience**: **`65%` (`26 of 40`)** of ordinary citizens delivered the maximum **`450-volt` ("XXX")** shock when commanded by a legitimate scientific authority at Yale.
