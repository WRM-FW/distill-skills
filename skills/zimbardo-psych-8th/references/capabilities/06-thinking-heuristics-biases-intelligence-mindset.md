# Capability 06: Cognitive Heuristics, Decision Debiasing, Intelligence Assessment, and Growth Mindset

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 6 (Thinking and Intelligence, `Core Concepts 6.1-6.5`, `Sections 6.1-6.16`, `Chapter Problem: What Produces "Genius"?`, `Critical Thinking Applied: Gender Differences in Cognition`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Components of Thought & Dual-Process Thinking (`6.1-6.4`)**: The brain manipulates **Natural Concepts** (fuzzy prototypes, e.g., `robin` for bird), **Artificial Concepts** (strict rule definitions, e.g., `equilateral triangle`), **Schemas/Scripts**, and **Visual Imagery / Cognitive Maps**. Nobel laureate **Daniel Kahneman's 2-Stage Model (`6.4`)** divides thought into **System 1** (fast, automatic, unconscious intuition relying on heuristics) and **System 2** (slow, effortful, analytical prefrontal reasoning).
  - **Problem Solving, Creativity & Decision Biases (`6.5-6.7`)**: Good thinkers choose between guaranteed **Algorithms** and fast **Heuristics**, overcome **Mental Set** and **Functional Fixedness**, debias the **6 Cognitive Judgment Traps**, and cultivate **Creative Genius** through deep domain knowledge (`10-year / 10,000-hour immersion`), intrinsic motivation, divergent thinking, and **defocused attention (`Shelley Carson`)**.
  - **Intelligence, Heritability, Mindset & Stereotype Threat (`6.8-6.16`)**: Modern **Deviation IQ (`M=100, SD=15`)** measures analytical/school abilities (`Spearman's g`), complemented by **Fluid vs. Crystallized (`Cattell`)**, **Triarchic (`Sternberg`)**, and **Multiple Intelligences (`Gardner`)**. Crucially, **within-group heritability (`~0.50-0.75`) cannot explain between-group IQ differences**, which are driven by socioeconomic environment, schooling, **Carol Dweck's Fixed vs. Growth Mindset**, and **Claude Steele's Stereotype Threat**.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Problem-Solving Strategies & Obstacles Matrix (`Ch 6.5`)

| Category | Mechanism / Strategy | Operational Rule & When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Algorithm** (`6.5.2`) | Step-by-step formula or exhaustive search | Guarantees a correct solution if one exists, but slow when state space is huge (e.g., chess, anagram permutations). |
| **Heuristic: Working Backward** (`6.5.3`) | Start from the known goal state and trace steps backward to the present | Use when the goal state is uniquely defined and has fewer branches than the starting state (e.g., water-lily doubling puzzle, project back-scheduling). |
| **Heuristic: Means-Ends Analysis** | Compare current state with goal state and eliminate the largest difference first | Use for complex multi-step engineering, planning, or troubleshooting (`Tower of Hanoi`). |
| **Heuristic: Creating Subgoals** | Break a massive problem into smaller intermediate milestones | Reduces working-memory load (`<= 7+/-2 chunks`). |
| **Heuristic: Finding an Analogy** | Map structural relationships from a solved domain to a novel problem | E.g., `Duncker's radiation tumor problem` (converging weak rays from multiple angles) or Velcro modeled on burdock burrs. |
| **Obstacle: Mental Set** (`6.5.4`) | Persisting with a previously successful strategy when a simpler or new rule applies | Countered by `Luchins water-jar` audit: explicitly ask *"What rule am I assuming that isn't actually required?"* (`Nine-dot problem`). |
| **Obstacle: Functional Fixedness** | Inability to see an object as having a function other than its customary one | Countered in `Duncker's candle problem`: mentally separate the box from its role as "tack container" so it becomes a "wall shelf." |

### 2. The 6 Judgment & Decision Biases Debiasing Protocol (`Ch 6.6`)

| Cognitive Bias | Psychological Mechanism (`System 1 Trap`) | Textbook / Real-World Example | Deterministic Debiasing Step (`System 2 Override`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Confirmation Bias** (`6.6.1`) | Searching only for evidence that supports existing beliefs while ignoring/discounting refutations (`Wason 2-4-6 task`). | Diagnosing a patient or vetting a candidate by asking only confirming questions. | Run a **Disconfirmation Test**: explicitly ask *"What evidence would prove my hypothesis wrong?"* and test negative rules. |
| **2. Hindsight Bias** (`6.6.2`) | *"I knew it all along"* effect: after learning an outcome, reconstructing past uncertainty as obvious and inevitable. | Blaming decision-makers after an unpredictable market crash or medical complication. | Record written probability forecasts and assumptions **before** the outcome occurs. |
| **3. Anchoring Bias** (`6.6.3`) | Over-relying on the first piece of numerical information encountered (`anchor`) and making insufficient adjustments (`Tversky & Kahneman`). | High initial sticker price on a car, salary negotiation opening offer, or arbitrary charity donation tiers (`$100 vs. $5`). | Generate independent baseline valuation bounds **before** viewing the counterparty's anchor, or explicitly anchor on the opposite extreme. |
| **4. Representativeness Bias** (`6.6.4`) | Judging the probability of an event/person by how closely it resembles a **prototype**, while ignoring **base-rate probabilities**. | Assuming a quiet, poetry-loving man is more likely an Ivy League classics professor than a truck driver (`ignoring the 1000:1 base rate`). | Always calculate the **Prior Base Rate** in the population before weighing stereotypic similarity. |
| **5. Availability Bias** (`6.6.5`) | Estimating event frequency/risk by how easily vivid examples come to mind (`memory availability`). | Fearing commercial plane crashes or shark attacks more than car accidents or heart disease due to vivid media coverage. | Replace gut vividness with objective **actuarial incidence tables** (`deaths per million hours/miles`). |
| **6. Tyranny of Choice** (`Schwartz`, `6.6.6`) | Excessive options (`e.g., 24 vs. 6 gourmet jams`) overload working memory, paralyze action, and trigger post-choice regret (**Maximizers vs. Satisficers**). | Decision paralysis and dissatisfaction in investments, career paths, or product catalogs. | Adopt a **Satisficing Rule** (define "good enough" criteria upfront), cap options at `3 to 6 curated choices`, and make choices non-reversible once settled. |

### 3. Intelligence Theories, Heritability vs. Group Differences, and Mindset (`Ch 6.8-6.16`)

1. **Psychometric & Cognitive Theories of Intelligence (`6.10-6.12`)**:
   - **Spearman's `g`**: General intelligence factor underlying positive correlations across cognitive subtests (`+ specific factors s`).
   - **Cattell's `Gf` vs. `Gc`**: **Fluid Intelligence (`Gf`)** (rapid abstract reasoning/pattern solving; peaks in early adulthood) vs. **Crystallized Intelligence (`Gc`)** (accumulated vocabulary, domain knowledge, wisdom; grows across the lifespan).
   - **Sternberg's Triarchic Theory**: `Analytical` (academic problem-solving), `Creative` (novel synthesis/insight), and `Practical` ("street smarts" / contextual adaptation).
   - **Gardner's 8 (or 9) Multiple Intelligences**: `Linguistic, Logical-Mathematical, Spatial, Musical, Bodily-Kinesthetic, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalist` (+ `Existential`).
2. **The Heritability Fallacy (`Lewontin's Seed Analogy`, `6.16`)**:
   - **Rule**: **Heritability** measures the proportion of variance *within* a specific group explained by genetic differences (`~0.50 in children, rising in adults`). Even if a trait is `100%` heritable within two pots of genetically identical seeds, if Pot A gets rich soil and Pot B gets poor soil, the height difference **between** Pot A and Pot B is `100% environmental` (`Scarr & Weinberg adoption study`, `Flynn Effect`, `Head Start`).
3. **Mindset (`Carol Dweck`) & Stereotype Threat (`Claude Steele`) (`6.16.7`)**:
   - **Fixed Mindset**: Believing intelligence is an innate, unchangeable entity -> leads to avoiding challenges, hiding errors, and helplessness after failure.
   - **Growth Mindset**: Believing intelligence and neural connections grow through effort, strategy, and challenge -> praise **process, effort, and strategy** (*"You worked out a great strategy on that tough problem"*), **never** fixed labels (*"You're so smart"*).
   - **Stereotype Threat**: Fear of confirming a negative group stereotype hijacks **working memory** with anxiety and self-monitoring. Counter via **Values Self-Affirmation Writing**, **Growth-Mindset Reframing**, and **Non-Diagnostic Test Framing**.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: What Produces "Genius" (`Ch 6 Opening Problem & 6.7`)

- **Problem**: Is genius (`Einstein, Darwin, Mozart, Picasso`) purely innate superhuman IQ (`Terman's Termites longitudinal study`), or how does it emerge?
- **Method & Result**: Terman's high-IQ children (`IQ > 135`) became healthy, successful professionals, but **none** became an Einstein or Picasso. True creative genius requires a threshold of intelligence combined with **~10 years of deep domain mastery**, **divergent thinking**, **defocused attention (`Carson`)**, **high intrinsic motivation**, and a **Growth Mindset** resilient to repeated failure.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Determine System 1 vs. System 2 Regime (`6.4.1`)**: Rely on **System 1 Intuition** *only* when the decision-maker has deep, feedback-calibrated domain expertise and time is short; otherwise force **System 2 Analytical Verification**.
2. **Break Problem-Solving Impasses**: Test for **Mental Set** and **Functional Fixedness**, then apply **Working Backward**, **Means-Ends Analysis**, **Subgoals**, or **Cross-Domain Analogies** (plus an incubation break to activate unfocused associative networks).
3. **Run the 6-Bias Checklist & Growth-Mindset Intervention**: Audit decisions against `Confirmation, Hindsight, Anchoring, Representativeness (Base Rates), Availability, and Tyranny of Choice`, and frame skill acquisition around **Dweck's Growth Mindset** and **Steele's Values Affirmation**.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Praising Children/Employees for "Being Smart/Talented", `Dweck, 6.16.7`)**: Labeling people as "gifted/smart" induces a **Fixed Mindset**, causing them to choose easy tasks to protect their label and collapse when hitting obstacles.
- **Failure Mode (Commercial "Brain-Training" Apps, `Ch 6 Psychology Matters, p. 226`)**: Consensus neuroscientific studies (`Stanford Longevity Center / Max Planck Institute`) show commercial brain games improve performance *only on the practiced game itself* with **zero far transfer** to general fluid intelligence; real cognitive enhancement comes from **aerobic exercise, learning complex real-world skills, and social engagement**.
