# Capability 07: Lifespan Development, Attachment, Piaget/Vygotsky, Erikson, and Moral Disengagement

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 7 (Development Over the Lifespan, `Core Concepts 7.1-7.4`, `Sections 7.1-7.14`, `Chapter Problem: The Jim Twins & Nature x Nurture Interaction`, `Critical Thinking Applied: The Mozart Effect`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Heredity x Environment Co-Action (`7.1-7.3`)**: Even identical twins reared apart (`Bouchard's Jim Twins`) reflect both shared genetic blueprints and similar cultural-historical environments. Prenatal development unfolds across `Germinal (0-2 wk) -> Embryonic (2-8 wk, organogenesis, highest vulnerability to Teratogens) -> Fetal (8-40 wk)`. Infancy relies on **Synaptic Overproduction & Pruning**, **Sensitive Periods**, **Harlow's Contact Comfort**, and **Ainsworth's Attachment Styles**.
  - **Cognitive & Psychosocial Stage Architectures (`7.4-7.14`)**: **Piaget** maps 4 qualitative shifts in schemas (`Assimilation` + `Accommodation`), complemented by **Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development (`ZPD`) / Scaffolding** and **Theory of Mind (`ToM`)**, while **Erikson** maps 8 lifespan psychosocial crises (`Table 7.2`).
  - **Adolescent Brain Pruning & Moral Disengagement (`7.9-7.11`)**: Adolescence features massive **synaptic pruning** and myelination where emotional **limbic reward/emotion systems mature faster than the prefrontal executive cortex**. Moral reasoning (`Kohlberg's 3x2 stages`) frequently diverges from moral action due to **Albert Bandura's 8 Mechanisms of Moral Disengagement (`7.10.2`)**.

### 2. Erikson's 8 Lifespan Psychosocial Stages (`Ch 7.6.3, 7.11-7.14`)

| Stage & Age | Psychosocial Crisis | Core Developmental Question | Positive Resolution (`Virtue / Outcome`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Infancy (`0-1.5 yr`)** | **Trust vs. Mistrust** | Is my world predictable and supportive? | Basic sense of safety and secure attachment (`Hope`). |
| **2. Toddlerhood (`1.5-3 yr`)** | **Autonomy vs. Shame & Doubt** | Can I do things myself or must I rely on others? | Self-control and bodily/action independence (`Will`). |
| **3. Preschool (`3-6 yr`)** | **Initiative vs. Guilt** | Am I good or bad at initiating play and plans? | Purposeful goal initiation without crippling guilt (`Purpose`). |
| **4. Childhood (`6-12 yr`)** | **Industry (Competence) vs. Inferiority** | Am I competent or worthless compared to peers? | Mastery of academic, social, and physical skills (`Competence`). |
| **5. Adolescence (`12-18 yr`)** | **Identity vs. Role Confusion** | Who am I and where am I going? | Integrated personal, vocational, and value identity (`Fidelity`). |
| **6. Early Adulthood** (incl. **Arnett's Emerging Adulthood `18-25`**) | **Intimacy vs. Isolation** | Shall I share my life deeply with another or live alone? | Capacity for committed love and deep friendship (`Love`). |
| **7. Middle Adulthood** | **Generativity vs. Stagnation** | Will I contribute to the next generation/society? | Mentoring, parenting, creative/civic productivity (`Care`). |
| **8. Late Adulthood (`65+`)** | **Ego-Integrity vs. Despair** | Have I lived a meaningful, authentic life? | Wisdom, acceptance of mortality, and life coherence (`Wisdom`). |

### 3. Kohlberg's Moral Levels vs. Bandura's 8 Moral Disengagement Mechanisms (`Ch 7.10`)

- **Kohlberg's 3 Levels (6 Stages) of Moral Reasoning (`7.10.1`)**:
  1. **Preconventional (`Self-Interest`)**: Stage 1 `Avoid punishment`; Stage 2 `Gain concrete reward / tit-for-tat exchange`.
  2. **Conventional (`Social Approval & Law`)**: Stage 3 `"Good boy/nice girl" interpersonal approval`; Stage 4 `Law-and-order social duty`.
  3. **Postconventional (`Abstract Ethical Principles`)**: Stage 5 `Social contract & individual rights`; Stage 6 `Universal ethical principles of conscience`. (*Carol Gilligan critique*: Kohlberg emphasized an ethic of **justice/rights** over an ethic of **care/responsibility**).
- **Bandura's 8 Mechanisms of Moral Disengagement (`7.10.2`)** (Explaining how good people commit cruel acts, bullying, cyberbullying, or corporate fraud without self-condemnation):
  1. **Moral Justification** (framing harm as serving a noble/patriotic/religious cause).
  2. **Euphemistic Labeling** (sanitizing harm with sterile words: "collateral damage," "team-building hazing," "downsizing").
  3. **Advantageous Comparison** ("What I did is nothing compared to what they did").
  4. **Displacement of Responsibility** ("I was just following my boss's/coach's orders").
  5. **Diffusion of Responsibility** ("Everyone in the group chat was doing it").
  6. **Distortion of Consequences** (minimizing or ignoring the victim's suffering, amplified by online anonymity).
  7. **Dehumanization** (stripping victims of human qualities, labeling them "vermin/losers").
  8. **Attribution of Blame** ("They brought it on themselves / deserved it").

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Zimbardo's "Demise of Guys" & Adolescent Risk-Taking (`Ch 7.11.2 & 7.9.1`)

- **Problem**: Why do adolescent teens engage in reckless driving/risk-taking alongside peers, and why do some young men struggle socially and academically (`Demise of Guys`)?
- **Method & Result**: Neuroimaging shows peer presence hyper-activates the adolescent ventral striatum (reward) before the prefrontal cortex is fully myelinated (~age 25); meanwhile, excessive solo immersion in fast-paced video games and online pornography rewires dopamine arousal thresholds away from slow, face-to-face classroom and romantic social contingencies.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Locate the Developmental Stage**: Map the individual's age and behavior onto **Piaget's Cognitive Stage**, **Erikson's Psychosocial Crisis**, and **Attachment / Temperament (`Kagan: Shy/Inhibited vs. Bold`)** profile.
2. **Apply Stage-Matched Scaffolding**: Use **Authoritative Structure + Warmth**, **Vygotsky ZPD Scaffolding**, and **Unstructured Play** in childhood; provide safe autonomy + peer-risk guardrails during adolescent prefrontal maturation; support **Selective Optimization with Compensation + Physical/Cognitive/Social Engagement** in late adulthood (`7.14.5`).
3. **Audit and Block Moral Disengagement**: Screen group/online behavior for **Bandura's 8 Moral Disengagement Mechanisms** (`euphemisms, diffused responsibility, dehumanization, victim-blaming`) and re-anchor personal accountability and victim empathy.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (The Mozart Effect Commercial Myth, `Ch 7 Critical Thinking Applied`)**: Playing classical music to infants does **not** raise their IQ; Rauscher et al.'s original finding was a `10-15 minute` spatial-task arousal boost in college students that media and politicians falsely extrapolated to infant brain development.
- **Failure Mode (Equating Moral Reasoning with Moral Behavior)**: Scoring at Kohlberg's Stage 5 on a hypothetical dilemma (`Heinz dilemma`) does not prevent unethical action if situational pressures trigger **Bandura's Moral Disengagement** (`7.10.2`).
