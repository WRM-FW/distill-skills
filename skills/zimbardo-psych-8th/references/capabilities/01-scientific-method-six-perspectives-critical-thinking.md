# Capability 01: Scientific Method, Six Psychological Perspectives, and Critical Thinking Audit

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 1 (Mind, Behavior, and Psychological Science, `Core Concepts 1.1-1.3`, `Tables 1.1-1.4`, `Figures 1.1-1.5`, `Critical Thinking Applied: Do Childhood Vaccinations Increase Risk of Autism?`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Psychology vs. Psychiatry vs. Pseudo-Psychology (`1.1-1.2`)**: Psychology is the empirical science of observable **behavior** and internal **mental processes** across 3 major domains (`Experimental/Research`, `Teaching`, `Applied [I/O, Sports, School, Clinical/Counseling, Forensic, Environmental, Geropsychology]`). Psychiatry is a medical specialty (`MD`) viewing disorders through a biomedical disease lens. Pseudo-psychology (`astrology, graphology, subliminal persuasion, fortune telling`) masquerades as science while relying on anecdotes, confirmation bias, and unfalsifiable claims.
  - **The Necker Cube Metaphor & 6 Main Perspectives (`1.3-1.6`)**: Just as the Necker Cube reverses depth perspectives via introspection, no single psychological viewpoint captures the whole truth. Every human behavior requires triangulation across six complementary perspectives.
  - **The Empirical Scientific Method (`1.7-1.9`)**: Science subjects falsifiable hypotheses and concrete **operational definitions** to objective pass-fail empirical tests while controlling observer/participant biases and enforcing ethical safeguards.

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. The 6-Question Critical Thinking Audit (`Ch 1.2.1`)

| Audit Step | Core Diagnostic Question | Red Flags (`Psychobabble`) | Scientific Verification Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Source** | Who makes the claim, what are their credentials, and what financial/political incentives do they have? | Self-proclaimed "gurus," celebrity endorsements, politicians scoring points (e.g., *Scared Straight* TV promoters). | Verify peer-reviewed domain expertise and conflict-of-interest disclosures. |
| **2. Claim** | Is the claim reasonable (extending established science) or extreme ("miracle breakthrough / effortless cure")? | Quick fixes for complex behavioral problems; claims contradicting basic physiology. | Apply Carl Sagan's rule: *Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence*. |
| **3. Evidence** | Is the claim backed by controlled empirical data or only vivid anecdotes and testimonials? | Hand-picked success stories; ignoring base rates and dropouts. | Require replicated experimental or controlled longitudinal data (`e.g., Petrosino et al. showing Scared Straight increases delinquency`). |
| **4. Bias** | Could **Emotional Bias**, **Confirmation Bias**, or **Expectancy Bias** contaminate the conclusion? | Remembering hits and forgetting misses (`astrology, lucky charms, sugar-high belief`). | Actively search for disconfirming evidence (`negative instances`) and use blind measurement. |
| **5. Fallacies** | Does the reasoning rely on contradictory "common sense" or the **Correlation-Causation Fallacy**? | Assuming `A correlates with B` means `A causes B` (e.g., ice cream sales & murder; vaccination timing & autism onset). | Test for **Reverse Causality (`B -> A`)** and **Third Variables (`C -> A & B`, e.g., summer heat or age of diagnosis)**. |
| **6. Perspectives** | Does the explanation reduce a multi-causal problem to a single narrow angle? | Treating juvenile delinquency solely as "lack of fear of punishment." | Triangulate across Biological, Cognitive, Behavioral, Whole-Person, Developmental, and Sociocultural levels. |

### 2. The 6 Psychological Perspectives Toolbox (`Ch 1.3-1.6`)

| Perspective | Core Causal Focus | Key Figures & Roots | Diagnostic Questions Applied to Procrastination (`Ch 1 Running Example`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biological** (`Neuroscience & Evolutionary`) | Brain structures, neurotransmitters, hormones, genes, epigenetic tags, and adaptive natural selection. | Descartes, Darwin, modern neuroscientists | Is there an inherited dopamine/reward-circuit sensitivity, executive prefrontal delay, or sleep-debt arousal deficit? |
| **Cognitive** | Mental representations, expectations, attention, memory encoding, schemas, and heuristics. | Wundt (`structuralism`), Gestalt, James (`functionalism`), modern cognitive science | Does the person fall prey to the **planning fallacy** (underestimating task duration), **confirmation bias** ("I always pull all-nighters fine"), or rationalization ("I work best under pressure")? |
| **Behavioral** | Observable stimuli and responses shaped by environmental reinforcement and punishment contingencies. | Pavlov, Watson, Skinner | What **immediate short-term reward** (gaming, social media relief) reinforces delay, and what last-minute completion rush (`negative reinforcement`) maintains the cycle? |
| **Whole-Person** (`Psychodynamic, Humanistic, Trait/ZTPI`) | Unconscious conflicts/defenses (`Freud`), self-esteem & growth needs (`Maslow, Rogers`), Big Five traits (`Conscientiousness`), and Time Perspective (`ZTPI`). | Freud, Maslow, Rogers, Allport, Zimbardo | Is delay driven by an unconscious fear of failure (`psychodynamic`), fragile self-esteem (`humanistic`), low **Conscientiousness**, or a **Present-Hedonistic** time zone (`trait/ZTPI`)? |
| **Developmental** | Predictable lifespan changes arising from heredity x environment interaction (`maturation, synaptic pruning`). | Piaget, Erikson, Vygotsky | Is adolescent prefrontal executive control still maturing relative to limbic reward circuits while task complexity spikes? |
| **Sociocultural** | Social norms, roles, peer conformity, authority, and cultural values (`individualism vs. collectivism`, clock-time norms). | Lewin, Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo | How do peer norms, ambiguous organizational deadlines, or cultural attitudes toward time shape task urgency? |

### 3. The 4-Step Scientific Method & 5 Research Designs (`Ch 1.7-1.9`)

1. **Step 1: Develop a Falsifiable Hypothesis & Operational Definitions**:
   - State a testable prediction capable of being proved wrong (never a pure value judgment like "sugar is bad", `Table 1.2`).
   - Specify **Operational Definitions** for every variable in concrete measurement terms (e.g., `Sugar` = `38 g in one 12-oz can of caffeine-free 7Up`; `Hyperactivity` = `trained observer score on a 1-to-5 activity scale`).
2. **Step 2: Collect Objective Data via One of 5 Research Methods**:
   - **Experiment (`Gold Standard for Causation`)**: Manipulate the **Independent Variable (`IV`)**, measure the **Dependent Variable (`DV`)**, hold extraneous variables constant, use **Random Assignment** to equalize `Experimental` vs. `Control` groups, and use **Placebo + Double-Blind Control** (neither participants nor raters know group assignment) to eliminate expectancy bias.
   - **Correlational Study (`r in [-1.0, +1.0]`)**: Measure natural covariation when manipulating the IV is unethical/impossible (e.g., lead paint exposure and IQ). Enables prediction, **never causation**.
   - **Survey**: Self-report questionnaires/interviews; requires **representative random sampling** and guards against social desirability bias and wording effects.
   - **Naturalistic Observation**: Observe subjects in natural habitats without interference; guards against **observer bias** and reactivity (`Hawthorne effect`).
   - **Case Study**: Deep longitudinal analysis of rare individuals (e.g., `Phineas Gage`, `H.M.`, `Jill Bolte Taylor`); rich hypothesis generator but low generalizability (`n=1`).
3. **Step 3 & Step 4**: Run statistical analysis (`accept/reject hypothesis`) and **Publish, Criticize, and Replicate** (`peer review` + independent replication).
4. **APA & Institutional Ethics Checklist (`1.9.2`)**: (a) **Informed Consent**, (b) **Protection from Harm and Discomfort**, (c) **Confidentiality/Privacy** (including Social Media Website `SMW` scraping rules), (d) **Full Debriefing** (mandatory if temporary deception is used), and **IACUC** oversight for animal research.

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Testing the "Sugar High" Myth (`Ch 1 Problem & Wolraich et al., 1995`)

- **Problem**: Parents strongly believe birthday-party sugar makes children hyperactive.
- **Method**: Wolraich et al. (1995) ran a 3-week **double-blind placebo-controlled experiment** comparing high-sugar vs. artificial-sweetener diets (`aspartame/saccharin`) with neither children, parents, nor raters knowing the condition.
- **Conclusion & Result**: **Zero significant difference** in behavior or cognitive function. Apparent "sugar highs" are driven by parent **expectancy/confirmation bias** and the exciting **situational context** of parties.

### Case 2: Debunking the Vaccine-Autism Panic (`Ch 1 Critical Thinking Applied`)

- **Problem**: Media panic following Andrew Wakefield's 1998 paper claiming MMR vaccine causes autism.
- **Method**: Applied the 6 Critical Thinking rules: tiny unrepresentative sample (`n=12`), undisclosed financial conflict of interest (paid by trial lawyers), **correlation-causation fallacy** (MMR is given around age 12-15 months, the exact developmental window when autism symptoms naturally become noticeable), and failure to replicate across millions of children (`Institute of Medicine`).

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Run the 6-Question Critical Thinking Filter**: Score any incoming claim across `Source`, `Extreme vs. Reasonable`, `Empirical vs. Anecdotal`, `Biases`, `Fallacies (Correlation != Causation)`, and `Multiple Perspectives`.
2. **Operationalize and Design the Study**: Define the `Hypothesis`, `IV (Operationalized)`, `DV (Operationalized)`, `Sampling & Random Assignment`, `Control/Placebo Condition`, `Double-Blind Protocol`, and `APA Ethics Safeguards`.
3. **Synthesize the 6-Perspective Matrix**: Map the target behavior across all 6 perspectives (`Biological, Cognitive, Behavioral, Whole-Person, Developmental, Sociocultural`) to prevent single-angle blind spots.

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode (Using Science to Settle Pure Value/Moral Questions)**: `Table 1.2` warns that empirical science cannot adjudicate non-falsifiable questions of ethics, aesthetics, religion, or ultimate meaning (though it can measure their behavioral consequences).
- **Failure Mode (Confusing Statistical Correlation with Causation)**: Treating a high correlation (`r = 0.70`) as proof of causation without random assignment and IV manipulation leads to wasteful or harmful interventions.
