# Capability 02: Biopsychology, Epigenetics, Neurotransmission, and Three-Layer Brain Mapping

## R - Source Anchor (Reading)

- **Source Location**: Chapter 2 (Biopsychology, Neuroscience, and Human Nature, `Core Concepts 2.1-2.3`, `Sections 2.1-2.10`, `Chapter Problem: Jill Bolte Taylor's Left-Hemisphere Stroke`, `Critical Thinking Applied: Left Brain vs. Right Brain`).
- **Core Mechanism**:
  - **Evolution, Genetics, and Epigenetics (`2.1-2.3`)**: Natural selection favors adaptive polygenic variations (`~20,000-25,000 human protein-coding genes`). Crucially, the **Epigenome** (`2.3`) acts as chemical "volume controls" (`methylation / histone modification`) that switch genes on or off in response to environmental experiences (maternal touch/grooming, exercise, nutrition, toxins, chronic stress) without changing DNA base sequences-and some epigenetic marks pass across generations.
  - **Dual Internal Communication (`2.4-2.6`)**: The **Nervous System** transmits fast, targeted electrochemical signals (`action potentials` + `synaptic neurotransmitters`), while the **Endocrine System** broadcasts slower, sustained chemical messengers (`hormones` via bloodstream, orchestrated by the **hypothalamus-pituitary master axis** and adrenal glands during stress).
  - **Three Evolutionary Brain Layers (`2.8-2.10`)**: Behavior integrates the **Brain Stem & Cerebellum** (autonomic survival, arousal, motor/cognitive timing), the **Limbic System** (emotion, memory consolidation, homeostasis), and the **Cerebral Cortex** (4 lobes, association areas, and lateralized hemisphere networks bridged by the corpus callosum).

## I - Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

### 1. Neural Signaling & Neurotransmitter-Pharmacology Matrix (`Ch 2.4 & Psychology Matters`)

- **Action Potential Sequence (`2.4.2`)**: `Dendrites receive input -> Soma integrates to threshold -> Resting potential (-70 mV) depolarizes via Na+ influx (+30 to +40 mV) -> All-or-None propagation down Axon (accelerated by Glial Myelin Sheath & Nodes of Ranvier) -> Terminal Buttons release vesicles into Synaptic Cleft -> Receptor binding -> Reuptake / enzymatic breakdown -> Refractory period`.
- **Glial Cell Functions (`2.4.3`)**: Outnumber neurons `~10:1` (or `~1:1 to 10:1` by region); form myelin, maintain blood-brain barrier, clear synaptic debris, and guide neural plasticity.

| Neurotransmitter | Primary Functional Circuits | Deficit / Undersupply Clinical Link | Excess / Oversupply Link | Agonist / Antagonist Drug Mechanisms (`Ch 2 & 8`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dopamine (`DA`)** | Voluntary movement (`nigrostriatal`) & reward/pleasure/attention (`mesolimbic`) | **Parkinson's disease** (motor tremor/rigidity); ADHD attention deficits | **Schizophrenia** positive symptoms (hallucinations/delusions); addiction sensitization | `L-DOPA`, `Cocaine/Amphetamines` (block reuptake = agonists); `Antipsychotics` (D2 antagonists) |
| **Serotonin (`5-HT`)** | Mood regulation, sleep/dreaming, appetite, impulse control | **Major Depression**, OCD, anxiety, impulsive aggression | Serotonin syndrome; hallucinogenic distortion (`LSD` acts at 5-HT receptors) | `SSRIs (Prozac, Zoloft)` block reuptake (agonists) |
| **Norepinephrine (`NE`)** | Sympathetic arousal, vigilance, mood, stress alertness | Depressed mood, low arousal/energy | Mania, panic/anxiety states, hypertension | `SNRIs`, tricyclics, stimulants increase synaptic availability |
| **Acetylcholine (`ACh`)** | Neuromuscular junctions, cortical arousal, hippocampal memory | **Alzheimer's disease** memory loss; paralysis | Severe muscle spasms/convulsions (`black widow venom`) | `Botox` (blocks ACh release = antagonist); `Curare` (blocks ACh receptors); `Nicotine` (ACh agonist) |
| **GABA** | Major **inhibitory** neurotransmitter calming CNS firing | Seizures, severe anxiety, insomnia, Huntington's tremors | Excessive sedation, coma, respiratory depression | `Alcohol, Barbiturates, Benzodiazepines (Valium, Xanax)` enhance GABA inhibition (agonists) |
| **Glutamate** | Major **excitatory** neurotransmitter driving **LTP** learning/memory | Cognitive/memory impairment, psychosis (`PCP/Ketamine`) | Excitotoxicity (neuronal death after stroke/TBI), migraines, seizures | Monosodium glutamate (`MSG`) sensitivity; NMDA modulators |
| **Endorphins** | Endogenous opioid pain suppression & euphoria (`runners' high`) | Hypersensitivity to pain, opioid withdrawal distress | Insensitivity to warning pain signals; respiratory suppression | `Morphine, Heroin, OxyContin` bind opioid receptors (agonists); `Naloxone` (antagonist) |

### 2. Brain-Scanning Selection Matrix (`Ch 2.7`)

| Scanning Method | Physical Mechanism | What It Measures (`Structure vs. Function`) | Best Clinical / Research Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EEG** (`Electroencephalography`) | Scalp electrodes record millisecond electrical waves | **Function** (`high temporal resolution`, low spatial precision) | Sleep stages (`NREM/REM`), epilepsy seizures, real-time ERP sensory responses |
| **CT** (`Computed Tomography`) | 3D X-ray slices through brain tissue | **Structure** (fast, inexpensive, uses radiation) | Emergency screening for acute stroke bleeding, skull fractures, large tumors |
| **PET** (`Positron Emission Tomography`) | Tracks radioactive glucose/neurotransmitter tracer | **Function** (metabolic activity & specific neurotransmitter receptors) | Mapping dopamine/serotonin receptors, early Alzheimer's metabolic decline |
| **MRI** (`Magnetic Resonance Imaging`) | Strong magnetic field aligns hydrogen atoms | **Structure** (`high spatial resolution`, no radiation) | Detailed anatomical mapping of gray/white matter, ventricles, small lesions |
| **fMRI** (`Functional MRI`) | Tracks blood-oxygen (`BOLD`) changes in real time | **Structure + Function** (no radioactive tracer, millimeter spatial precision) | Mapping active brain networks during cognitive, emotional, or social tasks |

### 3. Three-Layer Brain & Cortical Lobe Architecture (`Ch 2.8-2.10`)

1. **Layer 1: Brain Stem & Neighbors (`Reptilian Survival Core`)**:
   - **Medulla** (breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, reflexes), **Pons** (sleep-wake bridge, dreams), **Reticular Formation** (cortical arousal/attention filter), **Thalamus** (sensory relay station for all senses *except smell*), **Cerebellum** ("little brain": motor coordination, balance, procedural habit memory, and **8th Ed update: facilitating emotional, sensory, and cognitive timing; implicated in schizophrenia/autism**).
2. **Layer 2: The Limbic System (`Mammalian Emotion-Memory Ring`)**:
   - **Hippocampus** (consolidating new explicit/declarative memories and spatial maps; site of adult **neurogenesis**), **Amygdala** (fear/aggression appraisal, unconscious emotional memory), **Hypothalamus** (4 Fs: fighting, fleeing, feeding, mating; controls pituitary/endocrine system and reward pleasure centers).
3. **Layer 3: The Cerebral Cortex (`4 Lobes + Association Cortex`)**:
   - **Frontal Lobes**: **Motor Cortex** (contralateral voluntary movement, homunculus) + **Prefrontal Cortex (`PFC`)** (executive planning, impulse inhibition, working memory, personality-illustrated by `Phineas Gage`) + **Broca's Area** (left frontal speech production).
   - **Parietal Lobes**: **Somatosensory Cortex** (touch, temperature, pain, body position, phantom limb reorganization) + spatial tracking.
   - **Temporal Lobes**: **Auditory Cortex** + **Wernicke's Area** (left temporal language comprehension) + fusiform face recognition.
   - **Occipital Lobes**: **Visual Cortex** (color, shape, motion processing).
4. **Cerebral Dominance & Split-Brain (`Sperry & Gazzaniga`, `2.10`)**:
   - **Left Hemisphere**: Contralateral right-body control, right visual field; **analytic, sequential processing**, grammar, vocabulary, speech (`Broca's/Wernicke's`).
   - **Right Hemisphere**: Contralateral left-body control, left visual field; **holistic, spatial, musical, facial, and emotional prosody/context processing**.
   - **Split-Brain Rule**: If an object (e.g., a key) is flashed to the **left visual field** (right hemisphere) of a patient with a severed **corpus callosum**, the patient **cannot verbally name it** (left speech center didn't see it) but **can pick it up with the left hand** (controlled by the right hemisphere).

## A1 - Book Applications (Past Application)

### Case 1: Jill Bolte Taylor's Left-Hemisphere Stroke (`Ch 2 Opening Problem`)

- **Problem**: Neuroanatomist Jill Bolte Taylor suffered a massive hemorrhage in her left cerebral hemisphere at age 37.
- **Method**: Mapped her symptom progression onto lateralized cortical functions and tracked her 8-year recovery via **neuroplasticity**.
- **Conclusion & Result**: Loss of left-hemisphere circuits silenced her internal verbal monologue, speech (`Broca/Wernicke`), sequential time sense, and body-boundary ego, leaving her right hemisphere's holistic, peaceful spatial awareness dominant; intensive graded rehabilitation recruited intact tissue (`synaptic sprouting and cortical remapping`) to restore full professional function.

## E - Executable Steps (Execution)

1. **Map the Biological Level**: Identify whether the target phenomenon involves **Epigenetic/Genetic expression**, **Synaptic Neurotransmission** (`DA, 5-HT, NE, ACh, GABA, Glutamate, Endorphins`), or **Autonomic/Endocrine (`SAM/HPA`) activation**.
2. **Localize the Neural Circuit**: Trace the pathway across the **3 Brain Layers** (`Brain Stem/Cerebellum -> Limbic [Thalamus/Amygdala/Hippocampus/Hypothalamus] -> Cortical Lobe [Frontal/Parietal/Temporal/Occipital] & Hemisphere`).
3. **Select the Diagnostic Scan & Plasticity Plan**: Choose the appropriate imaging window (`EEG, CT, PET, MRI, fMRI`) and apply epigenetic/neuroplastic interventions (enriched practice, aerobic exercise, sleep, avoiding chronic cortisol/TBI).

## B - Boundary and Failure Modes (Boundary)

- **Failure Mode ("Left-Brain vs. Right-Brain" Personality Typology)**: `Ch 2 Critical Thinking Applied` debunks classifying people as "left-brained analyticals" or "right-brained creatives"; in intact brains, the corpus callosum transfers information in milliseconds so both hemispheres cooperate on every complex task.
- **Failure Mode (Genetic Determinism)**: Ignoring **epigenetics (`2.3`)** and **neuroplasticity (`2.4.4`)** by treating genetic predisposition or early brain injury as an unchangeable fate.
